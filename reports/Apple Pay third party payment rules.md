# Keep the merchant on the sheet, the human on the device

Apple Pay lets a third party carry a payment, but never lets it stand in for the person paying or for the business being paid. Apple's agreements recognize three roles. A **Merchant** processes payments under its own name and card-statement descriptor. A **Platform Provider** (a PSP such as Stripe, under a non-public addendum) registers merchants and acts "solely on [the merchant's] behalf." An **Intermediary Party** passes the encrypted payload to a merchant under strict data limits. Every new purchase still needs the payer's own Face ID, Touch ID or passcode on their own device. The only way to charge later without the payer present is a **merchant token (MPAN)**. An MPAN is bound to one card, one merchant and one user, and covers only the recurring, automatic-reload or deferred schedule the payer saw on the payment sheet. As of October 31, 2026, Apple has published no agent payment credential or agentic-commerce API, and none of the notes show Apple as a partner in Visa, Mastercard, Google or OpenAI/Stripe agent protocols. An AI agent can prepare a purchase and hand it to a human for approval. It cannot approve an Apple Pay payment itself. For Macoma, the compliant design follows from these rules. Owners' invoices run on Stripe-hosted pages as direct charges on each owner's connected account, so the owner is the merchant of record. Macoma sells its own plans and reply packs as the merchant, with separate Apple Pay authorizations for the subscription and the auto-reload. Apple Messages for Business is used only for Macoma's own sales, with staffed live agents. An iOS app follows Guideline 3.1, which differs between the US, the EU and the rest of the world. Several points remain unverified because Stripe's docs and Apple's Messages for Business policy pages could not be read directly. They are flagged below, and Macoma should confirm them with Stripe and Apple before launch.

## Apple lets platforms carry payments but bans a substitute merchant of record

Apple's contracts give a platform a defined lane. The Apple Pay Platform Web Merchant Terms (last updated March 10, 2023) define a **Platform Provider** as an e-commerce or payments provider the merchant engages under a written "Provider Agreement" that holds an active Apple developer account. Such a provider may use the Apple Pay APIs "solely on Your behalf and for Your purposes," and Apple can require the merchant to stop using a provider that breaches the terms ([Apple Pay Platform Web Merchant Terms](https://developer.apple.com/terms/apple-pay-web/)). The Direct Merchants version adds that a Platform Provider must have signed the **Platform Provider Addendum**, which is not public, and that it is an "Intermediary Party" under the developer agreement ([Direct Merchants terms](https://developer.apple.com/terms/apple-pay-web-direct-merchants)). Platform onboarding runs through the **Web Merchant Registration API**, which is open only to approved PSPs and e-commerce platforms. Each sub-merchant gets its own `partnerInternalMerchantIdentifier` and verified domains under the platform's shared certificates ([Web Merchant Registration API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applepaywebmerchantregistrationapi); [Apple Pay Platform Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/download/files/Apple-Pay-Platform-Integration-Guide.pdf)). Platforms that onboard this way "must also ensure these guidelines are being met by the merchant," meaning the acceptable-use rules ([Platform Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/download/files/Apple-Pay-Platform-Integration-Guide.pdf)). The merchant terms **end automatically when the Provider Agreement ends**, so an owner's Apple Pay acceptance through Macoma lasts only as long as their Stripe relationship ([merchant terms §9.1](https://developer.apple.com/terms/apple-pay-web/)).

The Developer Program License Agreement (updated October 29, 2026) defines the roles that matter for anyone in the middle. A **Merchant** processes Apple Pay payments "under their own name, trademark, or brand (e.g., their name shows up on the end user's credit card statement)." An **Intermediary Party** "passes an Apple Pay end user's Apple Pay Payload to a Merchant for processing." An Intermediary Party may use the payload only to facilitate that transaction and for its own order management. It must not keep the payload longer than necessary or combine it with other data it holds about the user. It must not use it "for advertising or marketing purposes, for developing or enhancing a user profile, or to otherwise target end users." It must name the Merchant on the payment sheet alongside its own name, and it must have a written agreement with each Merchant at least as protective of Apple as the DPLA. Any Merchant misuse is "deemed to have been taken by You." A party that only hosts a merchant checkout (for example, in a WKWebView) may "not access the Apple Pay Payload for any reason whatsoever" ([DPLA §1.2, §3.3.9(C)](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)). Apple is "not a party to any payment transactions" ([DPLA](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)). The merchant, or its Platform Provider, is "solely responsible for verifying the validity of transactions," and Apple accepts no liability for unauthorized ones ([merchant terms §12](https://developer.apple.com/terms/apple-pay-web/)).

The Acceptable Use Guidelines for Apple Pay on the Web set the categories and structures Apple will not allow. Sites may not offer tobacco, marijuana or vaping products; firearms, weapons or ammunition; illegal drugs; items that create consumer safety risks; pornography; counterfeit or stolen goods; or drug paraphernalia and sexually oriented services as a primary line. **Personal fundraising, nonprofit donations, and the purchase or transfer of currency, including crypto, are banned unless Apple approves them.** Most important for platforms, a site may not constitute "a staged digital wallet (e.g., where a second payment transaction is conducted in order to complete the first transaction or where there is a **substitute merchant of record** in a transaction)." Apple may disable Apple Pay on a site "for any reason it deems prudent" ([AUG](https://developer.apple.com/apple-pay/acceptable-use-guidelines-for-websites/)). Apple never defines "merchant of record" in the web terms. Read with the DPLA definition, though, the rule is clear: **the business named on the card statement must be the business actually selling**. A platform that charges the customer in its own name and then pays the real seller is the pattern the AUG prohibits. Credentials are also scoped. Merchants may not "repurpose or use recurring, subscription, automatic, deferred, or installment payment credentials for one-off payments," or use them "for transactions other than the transaction(s) for which it was originally authorized" ([merchant terms §3.3(o)](https://developer.apple.com/terms/apple-pay-web/); [Direct terms §4.4(i)](https://developer.apple.com/terms/apple-pay-web-direct-merchants)). Access to Apple Pay may not be shared or resold to third parties ([merchant terms §3.3–3.4](https://developer.apple.com/terms/apple-pay-web/)).

### The payment sheet must name the real seller and appear the instant the button is tapped

Apple's presentation rules are binding wherever they appear in the AUG or the terms, and they set the expected standard everywhere else. The sheet "must be presented immediately after the user taps the Apple Pay button, without any interim screens or pop-ups except to prompt for necessary product details" ([Apple Pay planning](https://developer.apple.com/apple-pay/planning/)). The total line reads "Pay [Business_Name]," using "the same business name people will see when they look for the charge on their bank or credit card statement." An intermediary must "identify both businesses," as in **"Pay [End_Merchant (via Your_Business)]"** ([HIG: Apple Pay](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-pay)). Line items explain fees, discounts, pending amounts and future payments, not individual products. Tips or other costs that come after authorization are disclosed as **"Amount Pending."** Subscriptions must state billing frequency, trial amount (including $0), regular amount and start date before the sheet appears ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-pay)). Buttons must be generated by Apple's API, must not be smaller than other payment buttons, and must not be hidden. The HIG includes a bill-pay button type for invoices from services "like plumbing or car repair" ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-pay)). Parity is a contract term. If a page accepts other third-party payment methods, Apple Pay must appear "at least on parity" with them, and if `canMakePaymentWithActiveCard` finds a card in Wallet, Apple Pay must be the primary displayed option ([AUG](https://developer.apple.com/apple-pay/acceptable-use-guidelines-for-websites/); [merchant terms §3.3(m)](https://developer.apple.com/terms/apple-pay-web/)). All Apple Pay websites need a privacy statement ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-pay)). Apple marks may not be used in paid advertising, including social media, without Apple's prior written consent, and may not appear in company or domain names ([Direct terms §9](https://developer.apple.com/terms/apple-pay-web-direct-merchants)). In apps, Guideline 4.9 requires all material purchase information before sale. For recurring payments it requires the renewal term, that the payment continues until canceled, what each period provides, the actual charges, and how to cancel ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).

## One Face ID approval covers one merchant, one disclosed schedule

Merchant tokens are Apple's only route to charging later. Apple's March 2026 integration guide says a one-time payment uses a device-bound DPAN. When a request includes a `recurringPaymentRequest`, `automaticReloadPaymentRequest` or `deferredPaymentRequest`, "a merchant token is automatically requested by Apple, and where supported by the issuer, returned." If the issuer does not support MPANs, Apple Pay "defaults back to returning the DPAN" ([Apple Pay Merchant Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/apple-pay/Apple-Pay-Merchant-Integration-Guide.pdf)). An MPAN "associates a payment card, merchant, and user" and survives device upgrades and resets ([Merchant token notifications](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applepaymerchanttokenmanagementapi/receiving-and-handling-merchant-token-notifications); [WWDC22 10041](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10041/)). Recurring and reload requests arrived in iOS 16 and macOS 13. Deferred requests arrived in iOS 16.4 and macOS 13.3 ([PKRecurringPaymentRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/passkit/pkrecurringpaymentrequest); [PKDeferredPaymentRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/passkit/pkdeferredpaymentrequest)). The launch networks were Amex, Discover, Mastercard and Visa. **Apple publishes no list of supporting issuers or regions**, so a backend must handle both token types ([WWDC22 10041](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10041/)).

Each request type has a fixed shape, and that shape sets the limit of what the payer approved. All three require a `paymentDescription`, a billing item and a **`managementURL`** where the user can update or delete the payment method. `billingAgreement` and `tokenNotificationURL` are optional ([ApplePayRecurringPaymentRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applepayontheweb/applepayrecurringpaymentrequest); [ApplePayDeferredPaymentRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applepayontheweb/applepaydeferredpaymentrequest)).

| Request type | What one approval authorizes | Apple's examples |
|---|---|---|
| Recurring | Charges at a fixed interval (unit plus count, start and optional end date). Optional trial. Fixed amount, or variable amount "when local regulations allow" | Subscriptions, utility bills, installments ([Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/apple-pay/Apple-Pay-Merchant-Integration-Guide.pdf); [HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-pay)) |
| Automatic reload | A fixed top-up amount whenever a **monetary** balance falls below a threshold | Store-card and prepaid top-ups, e.g. "$25 when balance hits $5" ([PKAutomaticReloadPaymentRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/passkit/pkautomaticreloadpaymentrequest); [WWDC22](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10041/)) |
| Deferred | One future payment on a stated date, with an optional free-cancellation date | Hotel bookings, pre-orders, car hire with incidentals ([PKDeferredPaymentRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/passkit/pkdeferredpaymentrequest)) |

Two structural limits follow. "You can only request **one type of automatic payment in a single transaction**," and "automatic payments cannot be used with multi-merchant payments" ([WWDC22 10041](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10041/)). A subscription plus an auto-reload rule therefore needs two separate approvals. The sheet total must be "the first amount that the customer will be charged" (which can be $0 for a trial). Only the first 500 characters of the billing agreement are shown, and that text "is not intended to replace your normal billing and legal agreements" or local recurring-billing law ([WWDC22](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10041/)). The HIG says a subscription change needs the sheet again only when it "results in additional fees." It follows that **price increases require fresh authorization** ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-pay)). No Apple type covers an open-ended "charge me for anything later" mandate. Together with the §3.3(o) ban on reusing recurring or deferred credentials for one-off payments, this means one Face ID approval cannot be stretched into general card-on-file purchasing. One processor, Paysafe, says outright that a saved merchant token may not be used for a new customer-initiated purchase ([Paysafe](https://developer.paysafe.com/en/api-docs/payments-api/add-payment-methods/apple-pay/apple-pay-recurring-transactions/)). Apple's public docs give no numeric limits on amounts, intervals or deferral horizon. They also do not say whether a deferred token may cover incidentals beyond the disclosed amount.

The merchant must assume a token can disappear at any time. Since WWDC25, users see every preauthorized payment in a dedicated Wallet view and can get notified before upcoming charges. A merchant that does not supply Merchant Token Usage Information shows up there only under "the default merchant name that comes from the payment network" ([WWDC25 201](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/201/)). Apple uses a tap-and-pull model for lifecycle events. It sends a GET to the merchant's `tokenNotificationURL` with an `eventId`, retries up to three times if the response is not 200, and keeps event details available for **seven days**. Events include card-expiry changes and `UNLINK` when the user or issuer deletes the token. Apple's guidance on `UNLINK` is to "trigger a notification to the customer to update their payment details before the next billing cycle" ([Merchant Token Management API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/merchanttokennotificationservices); [Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/apple-pay/Apple-Pay-Merchant-Integration-Guide.pdf)). Merchants must call **Invalidate a Merchant Token** when a customer cancels or switches payment method ([Invalidate a Merchant Token](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/merchanttokennotificationservices/unlinking-merchanttoken)). They must also honor network and Apple signals that a credential is inactive ([merchant terms §3.3(n)](https://developer.apple.com/terms/apple-pay-web/)).

On Stripe, search-result snippets of Stripe's docs say the Express Checkout Element and Payment Element accept `applePay.recurringPaymentRequest`, `deferredPaymentRequest` and `automaticReloadPaymentRequest`. They also say "Stripe automatically handles merchant token requests in Stripe Checkout," and that recurring Apple Pay should use PaymentIntents or SetupIntents rather than the legacy Charges API ([Stripe: Apple Pay merchant tokens](https://docs.stripe.com/apple-pay/merchant-tokens); [Stripe: Apple Pay recurring](https://docs.stripe.com/apple-pay/apple-pay-recurring)). **Three Stripe questions are open.** No source shows how MPANs bind under Connect (direct vs destination vs `on_behalf_of`). None shows whether Stripe relays Apple's `UNLINK` and card-update events as webhooks. None shows whether Stripe registers Apple's `tokenNotificationURL` for you. Macoma should get written answers from Stripe before relying on MPANs for connected-account billing.

## No AI agent can tap Apple Pay, and Apple has opened no agent channel

The device rule is absolute for new purchases. Apple says every Apple Pay purchase requires Face ID, Touch ID or a passcode. The exceptions, Express Mode for transit cards, student IDs and keys, apply at physical readers ([Apple Pay](https://apple.com/au/apple-pay); [HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-pay)). In third-party browsers the payer still authorizes on "an Apple Pay-capable iPhone running iOS 18 or newer" ([Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/apple-pay/Apple-Pay-Merchant-Integration-Guide.pdf)). A "reply YES" by SMS is not Apple Pay authentication, and neither is a confirmation inside an AI chat.

Paying "on someone's behalf at another merchant" fails on two levels. Cryptographically, the payment token is encrypted to one merchant's payment-processing certificate, so only that merchant or its processor can decrypt it. A token captured by an app is useless anywhere else ([Basis Theory](https://developers.basistheory.com/docs/guides/apple-pay/own-encryption-keys); [Apple: Payment token format](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/passkit/payment-token-format-reference)). Contractually, the workaround would be for Macoma to charge the owner as merchant and then pay the supplier. That is the staged-wallet, substitute-merchant-of-record structure the AUG bans ([AUG](https://developer.apple.com/apple-pay/acceptable-use-guidelines-for-websites/)). The notes also flag it as a likely money-transmission problem that needs counsel. Loading a balance for an agent to spend runs into the currency-transfer ban unless Apple approves ([AUG](https://developer.apple.com/apple-pay/acceptable-use-guidelines-for-websites/)). The one sanctioned way to carry a token looks like Stripe's Universal Commerce Protocol handler. Per a search snippet, the agent or platform shows the sheet for the *merchant's* identity, receives an "encrypted PKPaymentToken from Apple rather than raw card details," and passes it unopened to that merchant's endpoint ([Stripe UCP payments handler](https://docs.stripe.com/agentic-commerce/protocols/ucp/stripe-payments-handler)). The payer still authenticates on their own device, so this works only where Macoma has a web or app surface, not over plain SMS.

Apple's position as of October 2026 is visible mostly in what is absent. The notes found **no Apple newsroom, developer or WWDC announcement of agent payment credentials or an Apple Pay agent API**. A search of Apple's terms found no AI- or agent-specific clause ([AUG](https://developer.apple.com/apple-pay/acceptable-use-guidelines-for-websites/); [DPLA](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)). WWDC 2026 introduced a rebuilt "Siri AI" that works across apps, but the coverage reviewed says nothing about it paying through Apple Pay ([Variety](https://variety.com/2026/digital/news/apple-siri-ai-wwdc26-announcements-1236769265/); [MartianCraft recap](http://martiancraft.com/blog/2026/06/wwdc-26-recap-siri-grows-up-apple-intelligence-goes-everywhere-and-agents-move-Into-xcode/)). Apple does not appear among the published supporters of Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol, Google's AP2 or Mastercard Agent Pay ([Payment Expert](https://paymentexpert.com/2025/10/15/visa-ai-race-giants-compete-control/); [PYMNTS on AP2](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=3042771); [Mastercard Agent Pay release](https://s25.q4cdn.com/479285134/files/doc_news/Mastercard-Unveils-Agent-Pay-Pioneering-Agentic-Payments-Technology-to-Power-Commerce-in-the-Age-of-AI-2025.pdf)). Absence from public lists does not prove Apple has no private talks with the networks. Still, the operating rule today is that Apple Pay assumes a human present and authenticated at checkout. The network agent programs are separate products. They issue agent-specific tokens, built on the same Visa and Mastercard token services as Apple Pay, enrolled through the issuer or network rather than through Wallet, and authenticated with passkeys ([Visa Intelligent Commerce](https://developer.visa.com/use-cases/visa-intelligent-commerce-for-agents)). Stripe's Shared Payment Tokens follow the ACP delegated-payment spec: single-use, scoped to one merchant, capped by a maximum amount and an expiry ([Stripe Agentic Commerce Suite](https://stripe.com/newsroom/news/agentic-commerce-suite); [ACP Delegated Payment spec](https://agentic-commerce-protocol.pages.dev/docs/commerce/specs/payment)). If Macoma wants payments with no tap, it has to use these non-Apple-Pay rails and must not label them Apple Pay.

## Messages for Business suits Macoma's own sales, not owners' invoices

Apple Messages for Business is customer-initiated by design. Apple's policy appendix says the business "is prohibited from sending unsolicited messages." The exceptions are account and transaction notifications carrying an unsubscribe notice, Apple-approved Business Updates templates sent to subscribed phone numbers (iOS 18+), and, from 2026, Invitations to customers who already opted in ([Apple Appendix A—Policies](https://register.apple.com/resources/messages/messaging-documentation/policies); [Apple: Messages for Business & Privacy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/messages-for-business/); [Mobile Ecosystem Forum](https://mobileecosystemforum.com/2026/03/30/apples-business-messaging-invitations-a-new-messaging-experience-for-ios/)). For an AI-first company, the most consequential rules are these: "The business must provide **access to a live agent** in this channel during its regular business hours" and "must not provide a limited or **bot-only** solution" ([CM.com](https://knowledgecenter.cm.com/kc/do-i-need-a-human-agent-with-apple-messages-for-bu), quoting [Appendix A](https://register.apple.com/resources/messages/messaging-documentation/policies)). Apple's UX guidance asks businesses to disclose automation ("This is an automated agent"). A human must be reachable when the customer types "help," and the handoff should come with a routing message and a live agent who introduces themselves, without the customer having to start over. After hours, an auto-reply should say when a human will respond. Apple's pre-launch Experience Review tests for these points ([Apple UX design](https://register.apple.com/resources/messages/messaging-documentation/ux-design); [LiveChat: Experience Review](https://help.livechat-integrations.com/apple-messages-for-business/apple-experience-review)). Apple passes the business an opaque per-chat ID rather than the customer's identity ([Apple privacy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/messages-for-business/)). Prohibited participants include personal fundraising or donation collection without approval, and Apple says it is not currently onboarding businesses that primarily offer medical or health services ([Appendix A](https://register.apple.com/resources/messages/messaging-documentation/policies)). A search summary describes the appendix as "version 3.1, updated July 21, 2026" and lists a ban on "gaming involving purchases of digital goods." That version, date and wording are unverified.

The Apple Pay message type has its own requirements. The business needs an **Apple Pay Merchant ID**, a Payment Processing certificate (which expires every 25 months), and a Merchant Identity TLS certificate. It must request a **fresh merchant session for every payment**, using `initiative: "messaging"` with the gateway URL as `initiativeContext`. Sessions expire after five minutes. It must host a required HTTPS **`paymentGatewayUrl`** that Apple calls with the token. Optional fallback, order-tracking and shipping-update endpoints must be implemented if declared, and the update endpoints must answer within 30 seconds ([Apple Pay tutorial](https://register.apple.com/resources/messages/msp-api-tutorial/applepay); [Requesting an Apple Pay payment session](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applepayontheweb/requesting-an-apple-pay-payment-session.md); [Apple REST API: Type Interactive](https://register.apple.com/resources/messages/msp-rest-api/type-interactive); [Infobip](https://infobip.com/docs/apple-messages-for-business/manage-optional-configurations)). The result comes back as an interactive message with a `state` field. Apple advises confirming the payment in your own payment system rather than trusting that field ([Apple Pay tutorial](https://register.apple.com/resources/messages/msp-api-tutorial/applepay)). Apple Pay in Messages is governed by the Apple Pay terms ([Service Terms](https://register.apple.com/tou/bca/latest/en)). Businesses must connect through an approved Messaging Service Provider. Apple charges nothing, and MSPs set their own prices. Bird, CM.com (which can also act as the PSP), Infobip, LivePerson, Webex Connect and Text.com document Apple Pay support ([Apple FAQ](https://register.apple.com/resources/messages/messaging-documentation/faq); [Bird](https://bird.com/en-au/explained/apple-messages/what-does-apple-pay-in-messages-need-from-a-provider); [CM.com PSP API](https://developers.cm.com/payments-platform/v22.07/reference/psp-api-apple-messages-for-business-details)). Brand review takes a few business days, and Experience Review usually takes one to three rounds, so plan for weeks ([Apple: MSP Registration](https://register.apple.com/resources/messages/msp-onboarding/mspRegistration); [Bird: brand review](https://bird.com/explained/apple-messages/what-does-apples-brand-review-involve)).

The account structure shows who the channel is for. Apple allows **one commercial account per business** "for all use cases and business lines." Each account can link **only one Apple Pay Merchant ID**, although one Merchant ID can serve several accounts ([Apple FAQ](https://register.apple.com/resources/messages/messaging-documentation/faq); [Apple Pay tutorial](https://register.apple.com/resources/messages/msp-api-tutorial/applepay)). Both Apple pages date from roughly 2022–2023, and Infobip describes separate brand or staging accounts as optional ([Infobip](https://www.infobip.com/docs/apple-messages-for-business/get-started)). The model assumes that the brand in the chat is the merchant of record. That fits Macoma selling its own plans and reply packs to owners under one Macoma brand and one Macoma Merchant ID. The notes found no route for one account to collect payments for many unrelated businesses, and Apple's platform-integrator program covers only the web. Collecting owners' invoices through Macoma's Messages account would combine the substitute-merchant problem with the one-business-per-account rule. That traffic belongs on SMS or RCS with Stripe-hosted links. The notes found no evidence either way on whether the Messages Apple Pay payload supports recurring or reload requests (merchant tokens). Until an MSP or Apple confirms it, Macoma should sell one-off reply packs in the chat and send subscription sign-ups to Stripe Checkout.

## Stripe carries the Apple Pay paperwork on hosted pages, and an iOS app brings it back

On Stripe-hosted surfaces, Stripe is the platform integrator, so Macoma has no Apple setup to do. Apple's integration guide offers two onboarding methods. Merchant-owned developer accounts support native in-app Apple Pay but not hosted pages. The Web Merchant Registration API needs no merchant developer account, uses shared certificates, covers hosted payment pages, and "cannot be used for native in-app integrations" ([Platform Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/download/files/Apple-Pay-Platform-Integration-Guide.pdf)). Stripe's docs (seen only as search snippets) say "If you use Payment Links or hosted Checkout, Apple Pay works with no additional configuration," and list Connect and Invoicing as Apple Pay-capable ([Stripe: Apple Pay](https://docs.stripe.com/apple-pay.md?platform=web)). The notes infer that hosted invoice pages behave the same way, but found no explicit Stripe statement. Domain registration matters only when Apple Pay appears on a domain Macoma controls, through Elements or embedded Checkout. Then "the domain must be registered for that user, whether that user is your platform or the connected account." For direct charges that means per connected account via the API with the `Stripe-Account` header. For destination charges it means once, on the platform ([Stripe: Register domains](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/payment-methods/pmd-registration?dashboard-or-api=api)). Iframes need the top-level domain verified and `allow="payment"` ([Platform Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/download/files/Apple-Pay-Platform-Integration-Guide.pdf)).

The choice of charge type determines whether Macoma complies with Apple's merchant rules. With **direct charges**, the connected account is the merchant: its balance absorbs disputes, "transaction risk primarily affects connected accounts," and its name is the natural "Pay [Business]" label. With **destination charges**, the platform is the default settlement merchant. `on_behalf_of` moves the settlement merchant and (per a third-party blog) the descriptor to the connected account, but "disputes are debited from the platform balance with or without on_behalf_of" ([Stripe: Destination charges](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/destination-charges.md?locale=en-US&platform=web&ui=stripe-hosted); [Stripe: Connect disputes](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/disputes); [Stripe: Manage risk](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/manage-risk); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/mihirkanzariya/what-onbehalfof-actually-changes-on-a-stripe-destination-charge-and-what-it-does-not-3afm)). Apple has not said whether a destination charge without `on_behalf_of`, where the platform's name sits on the statement for a plumber's job, counts as a "substitute merchant of record." That is the reading most exposed under the AUG. Direct charges put the owner on the statement and on the sheet and leave dispute risk with the owner, which is the cleanest fit with Apple's model.

An iOS app changes two things: who holds the Apple credentials, and whether Apple's commission rules apply. Native Apple Pay requires **Macoma's own** Developer Program membership, Merchant ID, a Payment Processing Certificate generated from a Stripe CSR, and the Apple Pay capability in Xcode ([Platform Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/download/files/Apple-Pay-Platform-Integration-Guide.pdf); [Stripe: Apple Pay](https://docs.stripe.com/apple-pay)). If that app takes payments for owners' customers, Macoma is very likely an Intermediary Party. It would then owe the sheet label "Pay [Owner] (via Macoma)," the ban on combining Apple Pay data with customer profiles (a real constraint for an AI that builds customer memory), and written flow-down terms in the owner agreement ([DPLA](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)). In-app Apple Pay is limited to "goods and services that are to be used outside of any iPhone, iPad, or Apple Watch, unless otherwise permitted by Apple in writing." The DPLA also asks apps to make "commercially reasonable efforts" to include Apple Cash, and forbids decrypting payment data on the device ([DPLA §3.3.9(C)](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)). Opening the Stripe-hosted invoice page in Safari or SFSafariViewController avoids all of the native setup. Under the DPLA's WKWebView clause, a host that never touches the payload is not an Intermediary Party.

Guideline 3.1 then sorts Macoma's products into categories. Owners' invoices for plumbing, haircuts and similar work are services "consumed outside of the app," so Guideline **3.1.3(e) requires non-IAP methods "such as Apple Pay."** Macoma's own AI-assistant subscription is SaaS that unlocks functionality. That falls under 3.1.1 and 3.1.2(a), which name "software as a service." It does not fit 3.1.3(f), which is limited to VoIP, cloud storage, email and web hosting, or 3.1.3(c), which covers organizational sales "for their employees" ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Selling the subscription in the app with Apple Pay instead of IAP would breach both 3.1.1 and the DPLA's outside-the-device limit. The rules differ by region.

## A compliant Apple Pay design for Macoma

The design keeps Macoma in the role Apple's terms expect of software: it prepares and delivers payments, and the merchant and the payer stay visible. **Owner-to-customer invoices** go out as SMS or RCS links to Stripe-hosted invoice pages or Payment Links. They are created as direct charges on the owner's connected account, so the owner's name appears on the statement, on the "Pay [Business]" line, and on any dispute. Macoma never decrypts or stores the payload. If Macoma later embeds the Payment Element on its own pay domain, it registers that domain for each connected account. It also labels the sheet "Pay [Owner] (via Macoma)" wherever Macoma is visibly in the flow, which the HIG treats as best practice and the DPLA makes mandatory for an Intermediary Party. When an owner wants card-on-file for a customer, such as a monthly cleaning plan or a deposit with a later balance, the agent sends a Stripe Checkout link on the owner's account using the matching recurring or deferred request. The agent may only trigger charges that fall inside the schedule and amount the customer saw. Anything outside it ("charge them for today's extra visit") becomes a new payment link.

**Macoma's own revenue** runs on the platform account, with Macoma as merchant. The subscription is one Stripe Checkout authorization with a recurring request. Billing frequency, trial and cancellation terms are disclosed before the sheet, and the `managementURL` points to a page where the owner can change or cancel. The auto-reload of reply packs is a **second, separate** authorization with an automatic-reload request. It shows a fixed dollar top-up and a dollar-value threshold, because Apple's threshold is monetary, not a count of replies. Price increases trigger a fresh sheet. Lifecycle events (Apple `UNLINK`, card updates, and whatever Stripe relays) stop future charges and prompt a text asking the owner to re-authorize. Owner cancellations invalidate the token. Adopting Merchant Token Usage Information later would let Wallet show "Macoma" rather than a network default name, which should reduce "unknown charge" disputes.

**In Apple Messages for Business**, Macoma registers one brand and one Merchant ID with an MSP that exposes raw Apple Pay endpoints, such as Bird, Infobip, CM.com or Text.com. It sells one-off reply packs through the in-chat Apple Pay message, with a fresh merchant session for each payment and success confirmed by Stripe webhook before credits are granted. Subscription sign-ups go to Stripe Checkout until MPAN support in Messages is confirmed. Conversations are customer-initiated. Low-balance and renewal notices go only through Business Updates or transactional templates to opted-in owners. The AI assistant identifies itself as automated, and staffed humans take over on "help" during posted hours. **For purchases at other merchants**, the agent assembles the cart and deep-links the owner to the merchant's own checkout, where the owner taps Apple Pay on their device. Any future no-tap capability uses business-owned Stripe Issuing cards with spend limits, Shared Payment Tokens, or network agentic tokens. It must never be presented as Apple Pay and must be reviewed by counsel for money-transmission exposure. **An iOS app**, if built, opens Stripe-hosted invoice pages in SFSafariViewController rather than PassKit. In the US it links out to web checkout for the subscription. Elsewhere it uses IAP or carries no purchase calls to action.

## Conclusion

Apple has turned two long-standing payment ideas into a hard design boundary. The business on the card statement is the business that sold, and the human who pays approves on their own device. Agentic commerce is advancing on rails Apple does not run: network agent tokens, Stripe Shared Payment Tokens, and Issuing cards. Apple's silence as of October 2026 effectively places Apple Pay at the human-approval step of an agent's workflow rather than inside the agent. For Macoma, the agent's job is to remove friction before the tap and to manage scoped, disclosed authorizations after it. Any attempt to remove the tap or to stand in as merchant is where Apple's rules, the card networks' rules and money-transmission law all object at once.

The real risks are concentrated in a few places, mostly outside what Apple has published: how Stripe binds merchant tokens and names merchants under Connect, whether Messages for Business supports recurring payment requests, and where the US link-out fee settles after the Supreme Court and the district-court remand. Each of these can be settled with a written question to Stripe, an MSP or counsel, and each should be answered before Macoma builds card-on-file features or an iOS purchase flow. The rest of the design (hosted pages, direct charges, separate subscription and reload approvals, human-staffed Messages, and an agent that hands off rather than taps) runs on terms that are stable today: the 2023 Web Merchant Terms, the October 2026 DPLA and the June 2026 HIG.
