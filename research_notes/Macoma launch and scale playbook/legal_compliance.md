## 1. SMS/TCPA, CTIA, 10DLC/toll-free, quiet hours, state mini-TCPAs: what is required for (a) messages to the user and (b) messages to the user's customers

### Takeaway
Treat Macoma as running two separate messaging programs. (a) Messages to the owner are conversational or transactional texts the user started. They need a clear opt-in at web signup (with an OTP confirmation), STOP/HELP handling, and a registered 10DLC or toll-free campaign. Marketing to users needs separate prior express written consent, and it should never be bundled into the ToS the way Instinct bundles it. (b) Messages to the owner's customers carry the higher risk. The owner must hold the consent (written consent if the message is marketing). Macoma must enforce opt-outs across the platform, honor quiet hours, respect state mini-TCPA limits, and register a campaign that truthfully describes this use case. Carriers and Twilio will treat Macoma as an ISV/reseller.

### Cited Findings
**Federal TCPA consent rules (status as of Oct 2026)**
- The FCC's 2024 "revoke all" provision (47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)(10)) says an opt-out for one type of message covers all robocalls and robotexts from that sender. On Jan 6, 2026 the FCC's Consumer and Governmental Affairs Bureau delayed it again, from April 11, 2026 to **January 31, 2027** (order DA-26-12). The stated reason was to review the record from an October 2025 request for comment on modifying the rule. — [Burr & Forman](https://www.burr.com/telephone-consumer-protection-act/the-fcc-delays-effective-date-of-tcpa-revoke-all-rule-until-january-31-2027); [Privacy World, Jan 2026](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2026/01/fcc-extends-waiver-of-tcpa-consent-rule-providing-stop-one-means-stop-all/); [BHFS](https://www.bhfs.com/insight/fcc-extends-compliance-deadline-for-revoke-all-rule/)
- The rest of § 64.1200(a)(10) has been in force since **April 11, 2025**: consumers may revoke consent by any reasonable means (STOP, "quit", "end", "revoke", "opt out", "cancel", "unsubscribe" are all treated as reasonable), and opt-outs must be honored within **10 business days**. — [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/fcc-issues-one-year-limited-waiver-of-new-tcpa-consent-revocation-rule)
- The FCC's "one-to-one consent" rule for lead generators was **vacated** by the 11th Circuit on Jan 24, 2025 (*Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC*). The court held that it conflicted with the ordinary meaning of "prior express consent," which needs to be voluntary, clear, and given beforehand, but not limited to one seller at a time. — [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/250130-eleventh-circuit-vacates-fcc-s-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-rule); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/eleventh-circuit-vacates-tcpa-11-consent-rule). Some 2026 vendor guides still say the rule took effect Jan 27, 2026. That is wrong. — [Conduit/vendor summaries via search](https://conduit.ai/blog/a2p-10dlc-registration-step-by-step-for-2026)
- *McLaughlin Chiropractic v. McKesson* (U.S. Supreme Court, June 20, 2025) held that district courts are **not bound** by FCC interpretations of the TCPA and must reach their own reading of the statute. FCC orders are therefore persuasive rather than controlling in private TCPA suits. — [Supreme Court opinion, No. 23-1226](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/24pdf/23-1226_1b82.pdf) [background, verify]
- *Facebook v. Duguid* (2021) narrowed "ATDS" to equipment that uses a random or sequential number generator. Texts sent to stored contact lists are therefore usually outside the ATDS prong of federal law. The FCC's position that texts count as "calls," and the DNC/telemarketing rules under § 64.1200(c)–(d), still apply. — [Supreme Court opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-511_p86b.pdf) [background, verify]
- Marketing texts to numbers on the National DNC Registry require prior express invitation or permission, or an established business relationship. The FCC's 2023 order applied the DNC protections to text messages. — [FCC 23-107](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-23-107A1.pdf) [background, verify]
- The federal quiet-hours window for telephone solicitations is **8 a.m. to 9 p.m. recipient local time** (47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(c)(1)). — [Referrizer summary](https://support.referrizer.com/article/tcpa-quiet-hours-and-state-specific-rules-for-text-campaigns)

**State mini-TCPAs**
- Florida's FTSA (§ 501.059) was amended May 25, 2023 (HB 761). The amendments narrowed "autodialer" to an automated system for *selection and dialing*. Plaintiffs must now send a STOP reply, and the sender then has **15 days** to comply before suing over texts. Many online actions count as signatures for written consent, and companies are exempt when they have an established business relationship with the recipient or the recipient asked for the contact. — [McGuireWoods](https://www.mcguirewoods.com/client-resources/alerts/2023/5/pro-business-amendments-to-floridas-mini-tcpa-now-in-effect); [GT Law](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2023/5/florida-legislature-passes-bill-that-would-narrow-liability-under-florida-telephone-solicitation-act)
- Oklahoma's Telephone Solicitation Act (2022) allows solicitations only between **8 a.m. and 8 p.m.** local time and caps contacts at 3 per 24 hours on the same subject. Washington (RCW 80.36.390) also uses 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. Maryland's Stop the Spam Calls Act (2023) has the same window. — [Goodwin 2023](https://goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2023/02/02_28-mini-tcpa-laws-you-should-know); [Manatt](https://manatt.com/Insights/Newsletters/TCPA-Connect/State-Mini-TCPA-Telemarketing-Laws-Continue-to-P); [beancount 2026 guide](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/06/04/tcpa-a2p-10dlc-sms-marketing-compliance-2026-small-business-text-program-the-campaign-registry-consent-stop-help-keyword-state-mini-tcpa-florida-ftsa-texas-sb140-guide)
- Texas: as of 2025 the telemarketing registration law (Ch. 302, Bus. & Com. Code, per reports amended by SB 140) explicitly covers texts. Quiet hours are **9 a.m. to 9 p.m. Mon–Sat, and noon to 9 p.m. Sunday**. Registration with the Texas SOS may be required unless an exemption applies. — [Telnyx legal update](https://support.telnyx.com/en/articles/12141904-legal-update-texas-s-mini-tcpa-now-applies-to-texts). The bill number was not verified against primary text.

**Carrier rules: CTIA, 10DLC, toll-free**
- The CTIA Messaging Principles & Best Practices require consent appropriate to the message type: implied for conversational, express for informational, and express written for promotional. They also require opt-in confirmation messages, HELP/STOP support, and clear program disclosures (brand, frequency, "Msg & data rates may apply", links to terms and privacy). — [CTIA Messaging Principles & Best Practices PDF](https://api.ctia.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/230523-CTIA-Messaging-Principles-and-Best-Practices-FINAL.pdf) [background, verify current edition]
- US carriers **block** unregistered A2P 10DLC traffic. Vendor guides date the full block to early 2025. Twilio returns error 30034 for unregistered traffic. — [Apten 2026](https://www.apten.ai/blog/a2p-dlc-compliance-2026); [Conduit](https://conduit.ai/blog/a2p-10dlc-registration-step-by-step-for-2026)
- Twilio campaign registration now has dedicated **privacy-policy and terms URL** fields. — [Twilio changelog via search](https://static0.twilio.com/en-us/changelog?page=2)
- Since **Feb 17, 2026**, new toll-free verification submissions need a business registration number (EIN), except for sole proprietors. — [Twilio changelog](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/changelog/new-error-codes-provide-clearer-tfv-and-a2p-10dlc-registration-f)
- Since March 18, 2026, Twilio sets MMS throughput by Brand Trust Score. On Sept 15, 2026 it added more detailed rejection error codes for TFV and 10DLC. — [Twilio changelog Mar 17, 2026](https://static0.twilio.com/en-us/changelog/increased-mms-rate-limits-for-a2p-10dlc-phone-numbers-in-the-u-s0); [Twilio changelog Aug 6, 2026](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/changelog/new-error-codes-provide-clearer-tfv-and-a2p-10dlc-registration-f)
- Vendor reports (unverified) describe 2026 requirements for a reseller ID when an ISV registers on behalf of other businesses, and for an EIN at least 15 days old. — [Conduit](https://conduit.ai/blog/a2p-10dlc-registration-step-by-step-for-2026)

### Inferences
- **(a) Macoma to the user.** Use a single web signup checkbox that is not pre-checked and is separate from ToS acceptance. Suggested wording: "Text me from Macoma about my account and the tasks I ask for. Msg frequency varies. Msg & data rates may apply. Reply STOP to cancel, HELP for help. Consent is not a condition of purchase." Follow it with an OTP double opt-in and a compliant confirmation text. Register one 10DLC "Mixed/Conversational + 2FA" campaign, or a toll-free number. Keep OTP traffic on a separate campaign or number. If Macoma wants to market to its users (promos, referrals), collect a **separate** written-consent checkbox. Do not copy Instinct's ToS-embedded "marketing via autodialer" consent: it is weak legally and fails the CTIA "consent not a condition" expectation.
- **(b) Macoma to customers on the owner's behalf.** If these messages go from Macoma's shared number, carriers will see one brand sending many companies' content. That is the classic ISV problem. Options: (1) register **each business owner** as a brand with its own campaign, with Macoma as the ISV/reseller. This is the cleanest approach and matches TCR's model. Sole-proprietor brands are allowed with low limits. (2) Keep customer messages to replies within conversations the customer started. (3) Send the text from the owner's own phone or number, so Macoma drafts and the owner sends, for example through an iOS share sheet. This avoids most sender issues. LAWYER DECIDES, together with the deliverability team.
- Platform guardrails that hold up under any theory: a central suppression list, so a STOP from a customer number to any Macoma-sent message blocks all future agent messages to that number from that owner, plus a cross-owner flag; quiet hours enforced by the recipient's area code or time zone, using the strictest window (8 a.m. to 8 p.m.) by default; a frequency cap of 3 per 24h per subject per recipient to satisfy Oklahoma and Florida; a consent attestation per contact list ("I have permission to text these people"), with source and timestamp logged; blocking cold-outreach "lead" blasts; and per-message marketing classification so marketing messages need recorded written consent.
- Design to the stricter rules (revoke-all, 10 business days, any reasonable method) even though revoke-all is delayed until 2027. That avoids a migration later and is a selling point for the fair-terms positioning.

## 2. Who is the "sender" and who is liable when an AI agent texts a third party on a user's behalf?

### Takeaway
Under long-standing FCC precedent, the person who "makes" or "initiates" the message is usually the user who decides whether, when, and to whom it goes, not the app platform. That is true unless the platform is so involved that it effectively makes the call. An agent that *drafts and suggests* outreach pushes Macoma toward the "maker" side. Requiring the owner's YES per message (or per batch) is the key liability control. Since *McKesson*, courts decide this themselves, and the pending *Lowrey v. OpenAI & Twilio* case tests platform liability for AI outreach. Expect plaintiffs to name Macoma.

### Cited Findings
- FCC 2015 TCPA Omnibus Order: for app texting platforms (TextMe, Glide, YouMail), the "maker" is whoever takes the steps needed to send. A platform is generally not the sender when the user controls whether, when, and to whom texts are sent, but it may be when the platform is heavily involved, for example by choosing recipients or content. — [FCC 15-72, ¶¶ 30–37](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-15-72A1.pdf) [background, verify]
- Sellers can be vicariously liable under federal common-law agency principles for calls made on their behalf. — [FCC 13-54 *Dish Network* Declaratory Ruling](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-13-54A1.pdf) [background, verify]
- The FCC's Feb 8, 2024 Declaratory Ruling held that AI-generated voices are "artificial" voices under the TCPA, so calls using them need prior express consent. — [FCC 24-17](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf) [background, verify]. Practitioners say it is unclear whether this reaches AI-*written* texts. — [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/fcc-outlaws-ai-generated-voices-from-telemarketing-calls.html)
- The FCC's August 2024 NPRM proposed defining "AI-generated calls" and requiring AI disclosure at consent and on calls. Search found no final rule as of 2026. — [Inc./search summary](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/fcc-outlaws-ai-generated-voices-from-telemarketing-calls.html); NPRM at [FCC 24-84](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-84A1.pdf) [background, verify]
- *Lowrey v. OpenAI & Twilio* (W.D. Va. No. 6:25-cv-00116, filed Dec 29, 2025) is a TCPA suit over AI-generated robocalls and texts sent for a third-party company. It alleges the platforms could have required and enforced consent and DNC scrubbing but chose not to. Search found no merits ruling. — [DEV/practitioner summary](https://dev.to/alfredo_romero_fc4afdcb68/tcpa-after-lowrey-v-openai-what-ai-voice-agencies-must-change-right-now-1ec8) (secondary; check the docket)
- *McKesson* (2025) means courts interpret "make"/"initiate" without deferring to the FCC. — [Supreme Court](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/24pdf/23-1226_1b82.pdf) [background, verify]

### Inferences
- **Shared liability is realistic.** The owner is the seller and principal, so the owner is liable. Macoma could be a co-"maker" if the agent chooses recipients ("text all leads who haven't replied"), writes the content, and times the sends on its own. The YES gate, a recipient list the owner sees and approves, and a content preview all strengthen the "user made it" position. Removing YES for "autopilot" mode materially raises Macoma's risk.
- The ToS should say that the owner is the sender and is responsible for having consent, and require an indemnity from the owner for TCPA claims arising from the owner's contact lists. It is fair to make that indemnity **reciprocal**: Macoma indemnifies for its own platform failures, such as ignoring a STOP or sending outside quiet hours because of a Macoma bug. This is fairer than Instinct's one-way approach, and it is enforceable.
- Twilio's Acceptable Use and Messaging Policy makes the account holder (Macoma) answerable to Twilio for all traffic. Macoma therefore needs to pass those obligations down to owners in its AUP. — [Twilio Messaging Policy](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/legal/messaging-policy) [background, verify]

### Gaps
- No court has yet ruled on whether an LLM agent platform is the "maker" of texts it composes. LAWYER DECIDES on the risk appetite for any autonomous-send feature.
- Whether AI-generated *text* (not voice) counts as "artificial" under the TCPA is unresolved.

## 3. Privacy: CCPA/CPRA + ADMT regs, other states, FTC Section 5 and AI training, GLBA, children / 18+

### Takeaway
Macoma will hold sensitive personal information: message contents, email and calendar data, financial and invoice data, and contacts of third parties. That puts it under California and other state privacy laws once it passes the thresholds. Users can expect it to be held to strict FTC "no retroactive training" and "say what you do" standards. The CPPA's ADMT, risk-assessment and cybersecurity-audit rules phase in from 2027. The fair default is **no training on user content unless the user opts in**, which is stricter than Instinct's opt-out. That is legally viable, and Google's Limited Use rules nearly require it for Gmail data anyway.

### Cited Findings
- The CPPA board adopted ADMT, risk-assessment and cybersecurity-audit regulations on July 24, 2025. ADMT obligations (pre-use notice, opt-out, access for "significant decisions") apply from **Jan 1, 2027**. The first risk assessments are due **Dec 31, 2027**. — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/cppa-finalizes-ccpa-regulations-on-automated-decision-making-technology-risk-assessments-and-cybersecurity-audits); [Foley](https://www.foley.com/p/102kxz2/california-privacy-protection-agency-approves-draft-regulations-after-over-a-year/); [Willkie](https://www.willkie.com/publications/2025/08/cppa-approves-regulations-on-automated-decisionmaking-technologies)
- The final ADMT rules focus on "significant decisions" (financial or lending, housing, education, employment, healthcare). Sources expected the rules to take effect around Oct 1, 2025 after OAL approval, but the search did not confirm the OAL approval date. — [Sourcepoint](https://sourcepoint.com/blog/california-cppa-adopts-final-admt-regulations-as-netherlands-issues-human-intervention-guidance/)
- FTC Operation AI Comply (Sept 2024) went after deceptive AI claims. DoNotPay ("AI lawyer") paid $193,000. The final Workado order (Aug 2025) bars accuracy claims without "competent and reliable evidence." — [Frankfurt Kurnit on Workado](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102l358/ftcs-ai-crackdown-continues-workado-ordered-to-drop-unsupported-accuracy-claims); [NBC](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/ftc-cracks-down-on-donot-pay-others-for-deceptive-ai-claims-and-schemes/5828748/?amp=1)
- The FTC's Feb 2024 guidance warned that quietly changing terms or privacy policies to allow AI training on data collected earlier can be unfair or deceptive. — [FTC Tech blog "AI (and other) companies: quietly changing your terms of service could be unfair or deceptive"](https://www.ftc.gov/policy/advocacy-research/tech-at-ftc/2024/02/ai-other-companies-quietly-changing-your-terms-service-could-be-unfair-or-deceptive) [background, verify]
- The FTC has also ordered "algorithmic disgorgement," meaning deletion of models trained on improperly obtained data (e.g., *Everalbum* 2021, *Rite Aid* 2023). — [FTC Everalbum](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/192-3172-everalbum-inc-matter) [background, verify]
- The FTC has a 6(b) inquiry into companion and "relational" chatbots covering data inputs, retention and monetization. — [ABA Antitrust](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/antitrust_law/resources/newsletters/ftc-turns-up-heat-ai-enforcement-inquiry-messaging-from-the-top/)
- GLBA: the FTC Safeguards Rule (16 C.F.R. 314, amended 2021/2023, with breach notice to the FTC from May 2024 for events affecting 500 or more consumers) applies to "financial institutions," which include non-bank entities significantly engaged in financial activities. — [FTC Safeguards Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftc-safeguards-rule-what-your-business-needs-know) [background, verify]

### Inferences
- **CCPA thresholds**: $26.6M+ revenue (2025 CPI-adjusted figure; verify), *or* buying, selling or sharing personal information of 100k+ consumers or households, *or* 50%+ of revenue from selling or sharing. An SMS assistant can pass 100k quickly, especially because third-party customer contacts count. Build CPRA-grade rights (access, delete, correct, limit use of sensitive PI) from day one. About 19–20 states had comprehensive privacy laws in force by 2026 (VA, CO, CT, UT, TX, OR, MT, IA, DE, NJ, NH, TN, MN, MD, IN, KY, NE, RI…) [background, verify]. Several, including Texas and Nebraska, have no revenue threshold for small businesses that sell sensitive data. Maryland's MODPA (Oct 2025) imposes strict data minimization ("strictly necessary" for sensitive data). Use one rights workflow covering all of them.
- **ADMT**: Macoma reminding, drafting and invoicing does not make "significant decisions" about consumers. If Macoma ever scores leads or decides credit or payment terms for an owner's customers, revisit this. Risk assessments under the CPPA rules are also triggered by processing sensitive PI and by training ADMT, so a training opt-in program may require one. LAWYER DECIDES.
- **GLBA**: Macoma is probably not a GLBA "financial institution" just because it sends invoices through Stripe as a software tool for the owner. Stripe is the processor. Holding funds, extending credit, or moving money (x402) changes that. Even so, adopting a Safeguards-Rule-style written information security program (WISP) is cheap and expected by Intuit and Stripe partner reviews.
- **Children**: COPPA applies when a service is directed to under-13s or has actual knowledge of them. Asking for an 18+ attestation at signup plus the ToS minimum age is the industry norm and is legally sufficient for a general-audience adult product. Hard age verification is not required unless the product becomes a "companion" chatbot under CA SB 243 or CO HB 26-1263 (see §4). Fair alternative: same 18+ rule, but with a clear process to delete a minor's account immediately.
- **Fair training clause**: Opt-in training only, and never on connected-service data (Gmail, QuickBooks, Stripe), messages to third parties, or financial data. Zero retention for third-party (customer) message content beyond what operation and logging need. State plainly that Anthropic API data is not used to train Anthropic models under the commercial terms [background; verify Anthropic Commercial Terms]. This is viable, and it is stronger than Instinct's opt-out-except-Vault approach.

## 4. AI-specific laws (Oct 2026): Colorado, California, Utah, EU. Must the agent disclose it is AI when texting third parties?

### Takeaway
Colorado's original AI Act (SB 24-205) was **repealed and replaced** in May 2026 by SB 26-189, a narrower ADMT law effective Jan 1, 2027. Colorado also passed a **Chatbot Safety Act (HB 26-1263)**, effective Jan 1, 2027, that requires telling users they are talking to AI. California's SB 1001 bot law and Utah's AI disclosure law, as amended in 2025, require disclosure in commercial or sales contexts and on request. **Recommendation:** always identify AI-drafted texts to third parties (e.g., "— sent by Macoma, AI assistant for [Business]"). Doing so costs little, satisfies CA, UT and CO, and lowers deception and TCPA risk.

### Cited Findings
- **Colorado**: Gov. Polis signed **SB 26-189 on May 14, 2026**, repealing and replacing SB 24-205. The new law is built around "automated decision-making technology," drops the duty of care, risk program and impact-assessment requirements, takes effect **Jan 1, 2027**, and can be enforced only after AG rulemaking. Enforcement of SB 24-205 had already been paused by an April 27, 2026 stipulated order in xAI's lawsuit, in which DOJ intervened. — [Eckert Seamans](https://www.eckertseamans.com/legal-updates/colorado-repeals-and-replaces-its-landmark-ai-statute-what-businesses-need-to-know); [Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/colorado-replaces-landmark-ai-act-an-overview-of-the-new-sb-26-189-framework.html); [Carpe Datum Law](https://www.carpedatumlaw.com/2026/05/colorados-ai-reset-two-weeks-a-white-house-callout-and-a-pivot-away-from-the-eu-model/)
- Earlier history: SB 25B-004 (signed Aug 28, 2025) moved SB 24-205's effective date from Feb 1, 2026 to June 30, 2026. — [GT Law](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/9/colorado-delays-comprehensive-ai-law-with-further-changes-anticipated)
- **Colorado HB 26-1263 (Chatbot Safety Act)**, effective Jan 1, 2027, covers "conversational AI services," meaning AI systems open to the general public that *primarily* simulate human conversation. Requirements: disclose that the user is talking to AI (repeatedly for minors); do not imply output is equivalent to licensed professional services; maintain a suicide and self-harm protocol with annual AG reporting; meet duties toward minors. Violations are deceptive trade practices. The AG's draft rules (4 CCR 904-6) have a comment period reported to run through Oct 26, 2026. — [leg.colorado.gov HB26-1263](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/HB26-1263); [Akin Gump tracker](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/colorado-attorney-general-releases-interim-draft-rules-implementing-admt-and-chatbot-safety-acts); [Kiowa County Press](https://kiowacountypress.net/content/colorado-house-advances-bill-restricting-ai-chatbots)
- **California SB 243 (companion chatbots)**, effective Jan 1, 2026, requires AI disclosure, crisis protocols, protections for minors, annual reporting from 2027, and a private right of action. Bots used only for customer service or business operations are excluded. — [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/california-companion-chatbot-law-now-effect); [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/pdf/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai); [Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/ai-regulatory-update-californias-sb-243-mandates-companion-ai-safety-and-accoun.html)

- **Utah AI Policy Act**, amended by SB 226 and SB 332 (effective May 7, 2025): disclosure of generative AI is required only on a "clear and unambiguous request" from the consumer, plus proactive disclosure in "high-risk" interactions for regulated occupations (financial, legal, medical, mental-health data and decisions). Adds a safe harbor. The law is extended to July 2027. — [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/new-utah-ai-laws-change-disclosure-requirements-and-identity-protections-target); [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/chatbots-in-check-utahs-latest-ai-legislation/); [Davis Polk](https://davispolk.com/insights/client-update/utah-scales-back-reach-generative-ai-consumer-protection-law)
- New York (2025) also enacted an AI companion law with disclosure and crisis-protocol duties. — [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/pdf/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai)

### Inferences
- **Is Macoma a "companion chatbot" or "conversational AI service"?** It is a task and productivity assistant, so SB 243's business-operations exclusion probably applies. Colorado's "primarily simulates human conversation" test is closer for an SMS chat product, so assume HB 26-1263 *may* apply from 2027. Build the AI disclosure into the first message and the HELP reply, and keep a basic self-harm escalation response (988 referral). LAWYER DECIDES on coverage.

Allow an owner to turn off the AI label only where the owner has personally reviewed and adopted the text as their own (the YES flow). Even then, recommend keeping the business name and an opt-out line. LAWYER DECIDES on whether the owner-adoption exception is defensible.

## 5. Agency and contract law (UETA/E-SIGN), liability caps, arbitration and mass-arbitration trends

### Takeaway
UETA § 14 and E-SIGN § 101(h) already treat contracts formed by "electronic agents" as binding on the person who uses them. The user is bound, which is legally sound. *Fairness* comes from scoped authority (spending caps, confirmation for anything binding, an audit log) and from Macoma accepting responsibility for its own malfunctions. UETA § 10 gives consumers an error-correction right in automated transactions. Arbitration with a class waiver is still enforceable, but mass arbitration has made AAA and JAMS costly. Courts are scrutinizing "creative" anti-mass-arbitration protocols (*Heckman v. Live Nation*, 9th Cir. 2024).

### Cited Findings
- UETA § 14: contracts can be formed by the interaction of electronic agents, or of an electronic agent and an individual, even with no human review. UETA § 10 lets an individual avoid the effect of an electronic error in an automated transaction when the other party's agent gave no opportunity to prevent or correct the error, provided the individual promptly notifies and returns any consideration. — [Uniform Law Commission UETA text](https://www.uniformlaws.org/committees/community-home?CommunityKey=2c04b76c-2b7d-4399-977e-d5876ba7e034) [background, verify]
- E-SIGN, 15 U.S.C. § 7001(h): a contract is not denied legal effect solely because electronic agents formed it, provided the agent's action is legally attributable to the person bound. § 7001(c) requires specific consumer consent and disclosures before electronic records replace required written notices. — [15 U.S.C. § 7001](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7001) [background, verify]
- *Heckman v. Live Nation* (9th Cir. 2024) refused to compel arbitration under New Era ADR's mass-arbitration protocol, finding it unconscionable. Later decisions upheld AAA and JAMS mass-arbitration rules (*Carolus v. Coinbase*, N.D. Cal. Oct 2025; *Jones v. Starz*, 9th Cir. 2025). — [Mondaq/Privacy World 2025 year in review](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/12/2025-mass-arbitration-year-in-review/); [Troutman Feb 2026](https://www.troutman.com/insights/managing-mass-arbitration-risk-looking-beyond-provider-mass-arbitration-rules)
- AAA and JAMS adopted mass-arbitration procedures in 2024 (AAA covers 25+ similar cases; JAMS applies only by written agreement). AAA revised its Consumer Rules effective May 1, 2025. The AAA reported 92 mass arbitrations in 2024, totaling about 280,000 claims. Fees: AAA tiered, starting at $125 for the consumer and $325 for the business per case, decreasing with volume. JAMS consumer: the business pays most, with the consumer's share capped. — [ABA Business Law Today, Mar 2025](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2025-march/evolution-of-mass-arbitration/); [ABA Litigation](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/litigation/resources/newsletters/consumer/managing-mass-arbitration-risk-looking-beyond-provider-mass-arbitration-rules/). Note: secondary sources gave conflicting JAMS fee figures; check the current JAMS consumer minimum standards schedule.
- Drafting trends: informal pre-filing dispute resolution, claimant verification, and bellwether or batching clauses. Bellwether clauses have mixed results in court. — [Privacy World](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/12/2025-mass-arbitration-year-in-review/)

### Inferences
- **Instinct's "user responsible for all consequences + no meaningful human review"** is enforceable as allocation between the parties, but it is unfair and invites unconscionability arguments and FTC "unfair practice" framing when the agent malfunctions. **Fair alternative (viable):** (1) the user is bound by actions they authorize; (2) Macoma confirms before anything binding or costly (YES gate, default per-action and per-day spending caps, 24h undo or cancel window where the counterparty allows); (3) Macoma is responsible when the agent goes outside the user's instructions or Macoma's own controls fail, subject to the cap; (4) a UETA § 10–style error-reporting process; (5) a full action log available to the user.
- **Liability cap**: $100 or 6-months' fees (Instinct) is common. A fairer and still viable option is the greater of fees paid in the prior 12 months or $500–$1,000. Carve out Macoma's data-breach and indemnity obligations, plus its gross negligence and willful misconduct, which many states will not let a company cap anyway. Some states (e.g., NJ TCCWNA) penalize clauses that misstate consumer rights, so add "to the extent permitted by law" carve-outs. LAWYER DECIDES on amounts.
- **Arbitration**: keep it but make it fair. Small-claims option for both sides, Macoma pays all filing and arbitrator fees for claims under about $10k, a 30-day (or longer) opt-out, an informal 60-day dispute-resolution step, a batching clause that follows the AAA's own mass rules (rather than a proprietary protocol, given *Heckman*), and a public-injunction carve-out for California (*McGill v. Citibank*, 2017 [background, verify]). Fair alternative to consider: no arbitration, with small claims plus courts in the user's home county. That removes mass-arbitration fee exposure, but it reopens class actions, which is the main TCPA exposure. A business decision.
- **Non-refundable paid services** (Instinct): many states' auto-renewal laws (e.g., CA ARL as amended 2024 with click-to-cancel effective July 1, 2025) require easy online cancellation and renewal notices. The FTC's federal Click-to-Cancel rule was vacated by the 8th Circuit in July 2025 [background, verify]. **Fair alternative:** cancel anytime by texting CANCEL, prorated refunds for annual plans, and refunds for charges caused by Macoma error. Viable, and it lowers chargebacks.

### Gaps
- No 2026 appellate decisions on AI-agent contract attribution were found.
- Current JAMS consumer fee caps were not verified, because sources conflicted.

## 6. Payments and money transmission: Stripe invoices, holding funds, x402/stablecoins

### Takeaway
Invoicing through the **owner's own Stripe account** (Stripe Connect or the owner's OAuth-connected Stripe) keeps Macoma out of the flow of funds. Stripe is the licensed or registered party and the owner is the merchant, so Macoma needs no money transmitter license (MTL). Risk appears if Macoma ever **holds or moves user funds**, including crypto. Paying x402 fees from a Macoma-controlled wallet *on the user's behalf* with user-prefunded balances looks like custodial transmission. The GENIUS Act (July 2025) now governs stablecoin issuers. Search found no regulator guidance specific to x402.

### Cited Findings
- **GENIUS Act** (signed July 18, 2025) creates federal licensing for "permitted payment stablecoin issuers" (PPSIs), with reserve and BSA/AML/sanctions obligations. It preempts state money-transmitter licensing for qualified PPSIs but **not** state consumer-protection law. Treasury and FinCEN are creating a new BSA category for PPSIs. One secondary source says that after a 3-year transition, digital-asset service providers may not offer or facilitate unlicensed-issuer stablecoins. — [Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/genius-act-ushers-in-comprehensive-federal-regulation-of-payment-stablecoins); [Covington](https://www.cov.com/news-and-insights/insights/2025/07/the-genius-act-becomes-law-key-provisions-from-the-federal-stablecoin-regulatory-framework); [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/from-money-transmitters-to-ppsis-treasury-s-proposed-stablecoin-compliance-framework); [Flagright](https://www.flagright.com/post/genius-act-could-reshape-compliance-for-stablecoin-infrastructure-providers)
- FinCEN proposed amending the Travel Rule "transmittal order" definition to cover payment stablecoins. — [Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/blockchain-blog/the-genius-act-and-financial-crimes-compliance-a-detailed-guide.html)
- FinCEN's 2019 CVC guidance (FIN-2019-G001): **unhosted, non-custodial** wallet software providers are not money transmitters. A *hosted* wallet provider that accepts and transmits value is one. — [FinCEN FIN-2019-G001](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20Guidance%20CVC%20FINAL%20508.pdf) [background, verify]
- State MTL exposure depends on whether funds pass through the intermediary. The "agent of payee" exemption (in about 39 states) requires a written agreement with the payee, being held out as the payee's agent, and treating payment as received by the payee when the agent receives it. California DFPI has found that a software provider that only *sends instructions* between licensed institutions does not need a license. — [private.law wiki](https://wiki.private.law/en/money-transmitter-license-usa.md); [CA DFPI opinion](https://dfpi.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/337/2019/03/Sending-instructions-not-money-transmission.pdf)
- California's Digital Financial Assets Law requires a DFPI license for digital-asset business activity with CA residents, with licensing due by July 1, 2026 (previously delayed from 2025). — [CA DFPI DFAL](https://dfpi.ca.gov/regulated-industries/digital-financial-assets/) [background, verify]

### Inferences
- **Invoices**: use Stripe Connect *Standard* or direct OAuth, or create invoices in the owner's own Stripe or QuickBooks. Never collect funds into a Macoma account. If Macoma takes a fee on invoices, use Stripe Connect application fees, where Stripe handles the flow of funds. Apple Pay works through Stripe Checkout; it is a wallet on Stripe's rails and needs no separate license. The ToS should state that "Macoma is not a party to the payment, Stripe's terms apply," which mirrors Instinct's "not a party to purchases" and is fair and accurate.
- **x402**: safest designs, in order. (1) The user's *own* non-custodial wallet (e.g., a smart-contract or embedded wallet where the user holds keys or signs session keys), with Macoma only constructing payment requests. That matches FinCEN's unhosted-wallet guidance. (2) Macoma pays the fees *as its own operating expense* and bills the user in USD through Stripe, so Macoma is the purchaser and there is no transmission for others. (3) Avoid: user-prefunded custodial USDC balances spent by the agent. That is hosted-wallet transmission, triggering FinCEN MSB registration, state MTLs and California DFAL. Add per-transaction and daily caps, a merchant allowlist, OFAC screening of counterparties, and receipts by SMS. LAWYER DECIDES before launch.
- Prepaid "credits" spent within Macoma are generally closed-loop stored value (exempt from MTL in most states) when they are non-refundable for cash and used only with Macoma. That interacts with the "refund" fairness point, so LAWYER DECIDES.

## 7. Google OAuth restricted scopes (Gmail): Limited Use requirements for the privacy policy

### Takeaway
Reading or sending Gmail uses **restricted scopes**. That requires Google OAuth verification, an annual third-party security assessment (CASA), and strict Limited Use compliance. In particular, Gmail data may not be used to train generalized or foundation AI models, and no human may read it except in narrow cases. The privacy policy must include an affirmative Limited Use statement.

### Cited Findings
- Limited Use allows use of Google user data only to provide or improve user-facing features in the app's UI. Transfers are permitted only to provide those features, for security, for legal compliance, or in a merger or acquisition with prior consent. Humans may not read the data except with affirmative user consent for specific messages, for security purposes, for legal compliance, or for internal operations on aggregated and anonymized data. Selling data and using it for ads is prohibited. — [Google API Services User Data Policy](https://developers.google.com/terms/api-services-user-data-policy) (page failed to load during this session; content from search summary and [background, verify])
- Google's Application Use Cases guidance says the Workspace Limited Use rules bar using, transferring or selling user data to build or improve **foundational or generalized AI/ML models**, including aggregated, anonymized or derived data. Only personalized models for that individual user are allowed. Apps must post an **affirmative statement of Limited Use compliance**. — [Google Cloud Help: Application Use Cases](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13805798)
- Required disclosure wording (commonly used verbatim): "[App]'s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements." — [Karbon example](https://karbonhq.com/use-of-google-api-services/); [Google policy](https://developers.google.com/terms/api-services-user-data-policy)
- Restricted-scope apps must show they follow certain security practices. Search results did not describe the CASA tiers. — [Google Photos API policy](https://developers.google.com/photos/support/api-policy)

### Inferences
- Privacy policy must: list each Google scope and why it is needed; include the Limited Use statement; state no training of general models on Google data; state that Google data is sent to Anthropic (Claude) *only* to provide the user-requested feature. Treat the LLM provider as a subprocessor and confirm that it does not train on or retain the data. Describe retention and deletion of indexed Gmail and Calendar data, and how to revoke access via Google Account permissions.
- Minimize scopes: prefer `gmail.send` + `gmail.readonly` or metadata scopes over full `mail.google.com`. Calendar scopes are "sensitive," not restricted, so they need verification but not CASA. Budget time for verification and CASA (often weeks to months; cost varies by tier) [background, verify].
- QuickBooks (Intuit App Partner Program / security questionnaire) and Stripe Apps also have partner data-use terms. Mirror them in the policy [not researched].
