## 1. Stripe Billing mechanics: trial that ends on a date OR at a usage threshold, meters, credits, prepaid packs, overage, portal, Apple Pay, fees, dunning

### Takeaway
Stripe can do every piece Macoma needs (metered prices on Billing Meters, prepaid "billing credits", usage alerts via webhook, customer portal, Smart Retries, Apple Pay in hosted Checkout), but it has no native "end trial when usage hits N" switch and no native spending cap. Macoma has to build both: a usage alert webhook that ends the trial early, and its own hard stop in the app. Cost is 0.7% of billing volume on top of card processing.

### Cited Findings
**Trials and usage**
- Stripe trials end on a set date or timestamp, not on usage. Stripe doesn't bill usage recorded during the trial, but the usage shows in the meter event summary for the trial period, and billing for recorded usage resumes after the trial ends. — [Stripe: Use free trial periods on subscriptions](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/trials/free-trials); [Stripe: Configure trial offers](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/trials)
- A separate "trial offer" mechanism lets a metered price bill usage at an introductory rate during the offer. Stripe has a page comparing trial offers with free trial periods. — [Stripe: Compare trial offers and free trial periods](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/trials/compare)
- Free trials can be set up in Checkout. — [Stripe: Configure free trials for Checkout](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/checkout/free-trials)
- No Stripe page was found that combines a trial with a usage threshold. Search extracts concluded it must be built with webhooks or meter data. — [Stripe trials docs](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/trials) (absence finding)

**Usage alerts and thresholds (how to build the "usage ends the trial" and hard-limit logic)**
- Billing alerts notify you when a customer passes a meter usage threshold. API object: billing alert with `alert_type: usage_threshold`, a `gte` value, a meter ID and a recurrence setting (a one-time alert fires only the first time it is crossed). They can be per customer or for all customers. — [Stripe: Set up usage-based alerts](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/usage-based/alerts); [Stripe API: Alerts](https://docs.stripe.com/api/billing/alert)
- The webhook event is `billing.alert.triggered`. Limits: at most 25 alerts per meter-and-customer pair. Alerts don't work with test clocks. Evaluation counts usage reported before the alert was created. — [Stripe: usage-based alerts](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/usage-based/alerts); [Stripe changelog: Alerts API (2024-09-30)](https://docs.stripe.com/changelog/acacia/2024-09-30/billing-alerts-api)
- ~~A `spend_threshold` alert type appears in the notification list, but how it works was not confirmed.~~ [corrected in verification] Stripe's official OpenAPI spec (API version 2026-09-30.endive) has no `spend_threshold` anywhere. `alert_type` accepts only `usage_threshold`, `recurrence` accepts only `one_time`, and `filters` allows at most one filter (a customer). Don't plan on a spend-based alert. — [stripe/openapi spec3.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/stripe/openapi/master/latest/openapi.spec3.json)
- Usage "thresholds" on subscription items trigger an **invoice** mid-cycle (for example, when accrued usage reaches $100), not a stop. They can't be set in the Dashboard. — [Stripe: Set up thresholds](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/usage-based/thresholds?locale=en-GB); [Stripe: Scope your migration (Metronome)](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/usage-based/migrate-to-metronome/scope-your-migration)
- No native Stripe spending cap or credit-balance auto-recharge was found. Auto-recharge is described only for Metronome, a separate product hosted in Stripe's docs. — [Stripe: How Metronome works with Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/how-metronome-works-with-stripe)

**Prepaid credits (credit packs)**
- Credit Grants (billing credits) apply only to metered prices that have a Billing Meter attached. Each grant has a category (`paid` = purchased by the customer, `promotional` = free), a priority from 0 (highest) to 100 (lowest), and an optional expiry. With no expiry, the credits never expire. — [Stripe API: Credit Grants](https://docs.stripe.com/api/billing/credit-grant)
- Webhooks exist for credit grant created/updated and credit balance transaction created (added 2025-03-31). — [Stripe changelog: billing credit webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/changelog/basil/2025-03-31/billing-credit-webhooks)
- "Flexible" billing mode was added for subscriptions in the 2025-06-30 API version. — [Stripe changelog: billing mode](https://docs.stripe.com/changelog/basil/2025-06-30/billing-mode)

**Customer portal and dunning**
- The customer portal lets customers upgrade, downgrade, pause, resume or cancel subscriptions. A retention-performance view (private preview) tracks portal cancellations and "save rate". — [Stripe: Retention performance](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/retention/performance); [Stripe: Cancel subscriptions](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/cancel)
- Smart Retries are set in Dashboard > Billing > Revenue recovery > Retries. A custom schedule allows up to 3 retries. After retries fail you can cancel, mark unpaid, leave past due, or pause. Subscriptions auto-cancel after up to 8 failed attempts. — [Stripe: Automate payment retries](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/revenue-recovery/smart-retries); [Stripe: Revenue recovery](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/revenue-recovery)
- Stripe's own (marketing) recovery figures conflict: "57% of recurring payments that originally failed" on an older features page vs "55% of failed payments on average" on a newer page. — [Stripe Billing features](https://stripe.com/billing/features); [Stripe Billing (CH)](https://stripe.com/en-ch/billing); [Stripe blog: How we built Smart Retries](https://stripe.com/blog/how-we-built-it-smart-retries)
- Automation recipes include custom dunning by billing period, amount or segment (for example, up to 8 retries within 2 months, then cancel). — [Stripe: Automation use-cases](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/automation-recipes)

**Apple Pay for subscriptions**
- Hosted Stripe Checkout supports Apple Pay with no extra configuration. Embedded Checkout and Elements require registering every domain that shows the Apple Pay button. — [Stripe: Apple Pay](https://docs.stripe.com/apple-pay)
- With Checkout or Elements using the Checkout Sessions API, you don't need to configure Apple Pay merchant tokens yourself. A merchant token (MPAN) is tied to the customer, card and business rather than one device (DPAN), so it is more reliable for recurring charges. — [Stripe: Apple Pay merchant tokens](https://docs.stripe.com/apple-pay/merchant-tokens)
- With Elements' Express Checkout you can pass a `recurringPaymentRequest` to request an MPAN. For free trials Stripe recommends Subscriptions, because they create a SetupIntent that does a $0 validation, which avoids the cryptogram expiring before the first charge. — [Stripe: Manage recurring payments on Apple Pay](https://docs.stripe.com/apple-pay/apple-pay-recurring); [Stripe Support: Using Apple Pay for recurring payments](https://support.stripe.com/questions/using-apple-pay-for-recurring-payments)

**Stripe fees (Billing, Invoicing, processing)**
- Stripe Billing pay-as-you-go: 0.7% of billing volume, on top of processing. One write-up says it covers recurring billing, usage metering and dunning, and applies to volume billed on and off Stripe. — third-party: [UsageBox: Stripe Billing's 0.7% fee](https://usagebox.com/articles/stripe-billing-fees-2026-the-07-percent-math); [Flexprice 2026](https://flexprice.io/blog/stripe-pricing-breakdown-2026). Official page (not fetchable): [stripe.com/billing/pricing](https://stripe.com/billing/pricing)
- Monthly Billing plans from about $620/month at 0.67% above the tier are reported but were not confirmed on Stripe's page. Stripe changed its Billing Starter/Scale plans in 2024. — third-party: [Flexprice](https://flexprice.io/blog/stripe-pricing-breakdown-2026); [Stripe Support: Changes to Billing Starter and Scale plans](https://support.stripe.com/questions/changes-to-the-stripe-billing-starter-and-scale-plans); [HN discussion, Jul 2024](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40931486)
- Stripe Invoicing: Starter 0.4% per paid invoice, Plus 0.5% (adds quotes), and Custom. The fee applies only when an invoice is paid through Stripe and excludes processing and recurring-billing fees. — [Stripe Invoicing pricing](https://stripe.com/en-bg/invoicing/pricing); [Stripe Support: Invoicing pricing](https://support.stripe.com/questions/stripe-invoicing-pricing)
- US online card processing is 2.9% + $0.30 (Standard). — third-party 2026: [Flexprice](https://flexprice.io/blog/stripe-pricing-breakdown-2026); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/software/learn/stripe-fees)

### Inferences
- **Trial ends at N days or N units, whichever comes first.** Create the subscription with `trial_end` = day N, plus a one-time `usage_threshold` billing alert at N units on the Macoma meter. On `billing.alert.triggered`, update the subscription with `trial_end: "now"` to start paid billing immediately. Use `trial_settings.end_behavior.missing_payment_method` = `cancel` or `pause` for no-card trials. (Verified against Stripe's OpenAPI spec, 2026-09-30: `trial_end` accepts the special value `now` "to end the customer's trial immediately". `trial_settings.end_behavior.missing_payment_method` accepts `cancel`, `create_invoice` or `pause`.)
- **Hard limits must live in Macoma's own code.** Stripe alerts are asynchronous and only fire when usage is reported, so they can't block the next message. Macoma should keep a real-time counter per account (conversations, messages, token cost in cents) in its own database. It should check that counter before every LLM or SMS call, and use Stripe meters and alerts only for billing and notifications.
- **Credit packs.** Sell a one-time "pack" as a Checkout payment. Then create a `paid` Credit Grant against the metered price, with an expiry of about 12 months to limit liability. Use `promotional` grants for trial allowances or goodwill.
- **Overage.** Use a metered price on the same meter, so usage beyond the plan's included amount (or beyond credits) bills in arrears. Add a mid-cycle threshold invoice (for example, at $25 of overage) to cap Macoma's receivables risk.
- **Effective Stripe cost on a subscription**, based on the figures above: a $29/mo plan costs about $1.14 processing + $0.20 Billing ≈ $1.34 (4.6%), and a $49 plan about $2.06 (4.2%).
- **Apple Pay.** Use hosted Checkout (or a Payment Link) for the subscription sign-up. This gives Apple Pay with MPAN handling and needs no Apple domain setup, which fits a text-first product: text a Checkout link and the user pays with Apple Pay in Safari.

### Gaps

The page itself could not be opened.]

## 2. Stripe Connect platform fees on invoices sent for connected accounts

### Takeaway
Macoma can take a fee on every invoice paid through it by setting `application_fee_amount` on invoices. For one-off invoices it computes the fee itself; `application_fee_percent` is for subscriptions. The cheapest setup for a solo founder is direct charges on accounts where Stripe collects processing fees from the connected account (`fees_collector = stripe`, the Standard-style setup). In that setup the business pays processing and absorbs refunds and disputes, and Connect is reportedly free to the platform. With platform-controlled pricing (Express/Custom-style, or destination charges), Macoma pays processing plus $2 per active account and per-payout fees, and must build those into its fee.

### Cited Findings
- Connect invoices can use **direct charges** (create the invoice with the `Stripe-Account` header for the connected account, plus `application_fee_amount`) or **destination charges** (`transfer_data[destination]` plus `application_fee_amount`). The platform can keep a fee on either type. — [Stripe: Create invoices with Connect](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/invoices); [Stripe: Create an invoice with Connect](https://docs.stripe.com/invoicing/connect.md)
- `on_behalf_of` makes the connected account the settlement merchant and puts its branding and tax ID on invoice emails and PDFs. It must be set through the API before finalization, not in the Dashboard. The documented examples pair it with `transfer_data[destination]`. In a sandbox, `on_behalf_of` invoices don't send emails. — [Stripe: Create invoices with Connect](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/invoices); [Stripe: invoicing/connect](https://docs.stripe.com/invoicing/connect.md)
- With direct charges, the connected account bears Stripe fees, refunds and chargebacks. — [Stripe: Create an invoice with Connect (v1)](https://docs.stripe.com/invoicing/connect?accounts-namespace=v1)
- **Fee limits:** on direct charges the application fee must be positive and below the charge amount, and it is capped at the captured amount. Application-fee objects are created asynchronously; listen for `application_fee.created`. — [Stripe: Create direct charges](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges)
- **Accounts v2 / controller properties:** the fee setting is `defaults.responsibilities.fees_collector`, with values `application` or `stripe` (read-back can also show `application_custom` or `application_express`). The v1 equivalent is `controller.fees.payer`. [corrected in verification: in v1 the values are `account` or `application`, not `stripe`, per the OpenAPI spec.]
  - With `application`, the platform collects application fees from the connected account and Stripe collects payment fees from the platform. With `stripe`, Stripe collects payment fees directly from the connected account.
  - The setting applies only to direct charges. For destination and separate charges, Stripe always collects fees from the platform.
  - If `losses_collector = application`, then `fees_collector` must also be `application`.
  - When the platform is the fee collector, the application fee must also cover Stripe's processing fee.
  - Dispute fees are paid by the platform when `fees_collector = application`, otherwise by the connected account.
  - Sources: [Stripe: Fee behavior on connected accounts](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges-fee-payer-behavior); [Stripe: Configure connected accounts (v2)](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/accounts-v2/connected-account-configuration); [Stripe: Migrate to controller properties](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/migrate-to-controller-properties); [Stripe API: Accounts v2](https://docs.stripe.com/api/v2/core/accounts)
- Accounts v2 examples cite preview API versions 2026-03-25.preview and 2026-09-30.preview. — [Stripe API v2 Accounts (2026-03-25.preview)](https://docs.stripe.com/api/v2/core/accounts?api-version=2026-03-25.preview)
- **Subscriptions for connected accounts** support `application_fee_percent` (the example uses 10 with `on_behalf_of` and `transfer_data[destination]`), and it can be set after creation. Direct or destination charges both work. — [Stripe: Connect subscriptions](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/subscriptions)
- A separate doc covers charging SaaS fees to connected accounts (platform billing its users through Billing). — [Stripe: Charge SaaS fees to connected accounts](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/integrate-billing-connect)
- **Connect pricing:**
  - When the platform sets pricing for its users, Connect costs $2 per monthly active account (active = receives a payout that month) plus 0.25% + 25¢ per payout.
  - Connect is free to the platform when Stripe handles pricing for connected accounts.
  - Instant payouts are 1% (min $0.50) in most sources; one source says 1.5%.
  - Sources: official page (not fetchable) [stripe.com/connect/pricing](https://stripe.com/it-us/connect/pricing); third-party: [Sharetribe](https://www.sharetribe.com/academy/marketplace-payments/stripe-connect-overview/), [Fiska](https://fiska.com/blog/stripe-connect-pricing/), [Drop-desk 2026](https://drop-desk.com/blog/guides/stripe-fees-for-marketplace-operators/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended for Macoma v1:** direct-charge invoices on the business's own connected account (Standard-style: full Stripe Dashboard, `fees_collector = stripe`, `losses_collector = stripe`), with `application_fee_amount` = Macoma's fee. Benefits:
  - The business is the merchant of record and pays processing.
  - Macoma keeps the whole fee, avoids the $2/active-account and payout fees (per the third-party Connect pricing), and carries no refund or dispute liability.
- **Fee math for one-off invoices.** Invoices use `application_fee_amount` (a fixed amount), so a percentage fee (for example 1% with a $0.50 minimum and $25 maximum) must be computed by Macoma before finalizing. Example: a $500 invoice at 1% gives Macoma $5.00, and the business separately pays about $14.80 processing (2.9% + 30¢).
- **If Macoma uses Express or destination charges** (better onboarding UX, platform-branded), it pays processing itself. A $500 invoice then costs Macoma about $14.80 processing + 0.25% + 25¢ payout ≈ $1.50, plus $2/month per active business. So the fee must be roughly 3.15% + 55¢ per invoice, plus $2/month per active business, just to break even before any profit [corrected in verification: the earlier "3.5% + 55¢" overstated 2.9% + 0.25%]. That is uncompetitive with Jobber or Housecall Pro payments.
- The Stripe Invoicing fee (0.4% Starter) would likely be charged to the account that owns the invoice (the connected account under direct charges). I did not verify this; see Gaps.

### Gaps
- Not verified: who pays the 0.4% Stripe Invoicing fee on direct-charge Connect invoices (platform or connected account).
- Not verified: whether `on_behalf_of` is supported with direct-charge invoices. The docs found only pair it with destination charges.
- I found no Stripe rule capping platform fees beyond "below the charge amount". I also did not research Stripe's disclosure requirements to end customers, or state/card-network surcharging rules for passing fees to payers.
- Official Connect pricing numbers come from third-party restatements; stripe.com/connect/pricing could not be fetched.

### Inferences
- **Jobber's structure is the closest analog and a good template:** a small included allowance, a per-conversation overage, and a user-selectable "pause at cap".
  - Macoma could offer Starter ~$29–39/mo with ~100 conversations, Pro ~$79–99 with ~400 conversations and 2 seats, and overage at ~$0.50–0.79/conversation or prepaid packs.
  - Expansion revenue: +$15–25/mo per extra agent or team seat, plus a 1% (min/max-bounded) invoice fee.
  - These are proposals derived from the benchmarks, not sourced prices.
- **Define a "conversation" explicitly** (for example, one customer thread within 24h, capped at N messages). Fin's "resolution" definition drew criticism, so a transparent, countable unit reduces disputes.
- With industry gross margin at about 45–56%, a target of **≥60% gross margin per account**, enforced by a cost budget, would put Macoma above the AI-app average.

## 5. Subscription compliance: FTC click-to-cancel, California ARL, state trial-reminder laws

### Takeaway
No federal click-to-cancel rule is in force in Oct 2026 (vacated July 2025; the FTC restarted with an ANPRM in March 2026), but ROSCA still applies. California's amended ARL (effective July 1, 2025) explicitly covers free-to-paid trials, requires separate consent, and requires cancellation through the same medium as sign-up. For a text-first product, that strongly implies cancel-by-text. NY, MN and VA have trial-reminder windows for trials longer than about a month.

### Inferences
- **Compliance-by-design checklist for Macoma's trial-to-paid flow:**
  1. Before collecting a card, disclose clearly that the plan renews automatically, the price, when the trial ends (date or N conversations, whichever comes first), and how to cancel.
  2. Get a separate, unchecked consent click on Checkout. (Verified in Stripe's OpenAPI spec: Checkout Sessions take `consent_collection.terms_of_service: "required"` plus `custom_text.terms_of_service_acceptance`, which gives a required checkbox with custom text. Whether that one checkbox satisfies California's "separate from the terms" consent is a legal judgment.)
  3. Text a reminder about 7 days, and again 1–3 days, before conversion. This satisfies the NY 3–21 day window and is a safe default. For usage-triggered conversion, warn at about 80% of the trial allowance.
  4. Support cancel-by-text (for example, "CANCEL PLAN"), because sign-up happens by text and California requires the same medium. Also link the Stripe customer portal.
  5. Send a confirmation after the conversion charge and after cancellation.
  6. [corrected in verification] Send California subscribers an annual reminder of the terms (product, amount and frequency, how to cancel), on monthly plans too. See the AB 2863 correction above.
- **Keep SMS opt-out separate from subscription cancellation.** SMS keyword STOP (carrier/CTIA opt-out) is a different thing from cancelling the subscription. Handle both and explain the difference in the reply.
- **Keep trials at 30 days or less.** That likely avoids the separate trial-specific notice triggers in NY (>1 month), MN (>30 days) and VA (>30 days), though California's general duties still apply.
