## Q1. With Stripe (Connect, Checkout, hosted invoices, Payment Links, Payment Element): whose Apple Pay merchant ID is used, what domain registration is needed, and what is the platform responsible for (direct vs destination charges)?

### Takeaway
On Stripe-hosted surfaces (Checkout hosted page, Payment Links, and likely the hosted invoice page), Stripe is the Apple Pay platform integrator. Stripe handles merchant validation under its own Apple Pay credentials, so neither Macoma nor the owners need an Apple Developer account, a merchant ID, or a domain verification file. Domain registration matters only when Apple Pay appears on a domain Macoma controls (Elements/Payment Element, embedded Checkout). In that case the domain must be registered for whichever account runs the charge: each connected account for direct charges (API only), the platform for destination charges. The money side follows the charge type. Direct charges put the connected account in the merchant role: its balance takes the disputed amount, its descriptor is used, and risk sits mainly with it. Destination charges leave the platform liable for disputes and refunds, even with `on_behalf_of`, which changes only the settlement merchant.

### Cited Findings
**Merchant ID and credentials (Apple side)**
- Apple's Platform Integration Guide (cover dated September 2025, page footers June 2025) gives two ways to onboard merchants. (1) Each merchant uses its own Apple Developer Account and credentials. This "Supports native in-app Apple Pay integrations", requires the merchant "to have their own Apple Developer Account", and "Does not support Hosted Payment Pages." (2) The **Apple Pay Web Merchant Registration API**, where "Merchant is not required to create an Apple Developer Account", the platform has a "Shared set of keys and certificates across your Merchant Portfolio", "Merchants can register their own domains, hosted payment pages, or both", "Domain registration does not expire", and the method "Cannot be used for native in-app integrations." — [Apple Pay Platform Integration Guide (PDF)](https://developer.apple.com/download/files/Apple-Pay-Platform-Integration-Guide.pdf)
- Only "Platforms enrolled in the Apple developer program as an organization" can apply for the Web Merchant Registration API, and they must be approved. Approved platforms set up a "Payment Platform Integrator ID" and certificates. — [Apple Pay Platform Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/download/files/Apple-Pay-Platform-Integration-Guide.pdf); [Apple Web Merchant Registration API docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applepaywebmerchantregistrationapi) (search snippet: "Your developer account and your business needs must meet certain requirements to apply, and be approved")
- For hosted payment forms, Apple says platforms "should ensure that they enable onboarding merchants through the Apple Pay Web Merchant Registration API" and "should use the partnerInternalMerchantIdentifier value ... in all fields used for the Merchant Identifier." In other words, each sub-merchant gets its own platform-scoped merchant identifier. — [Apple Pay Platform Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/download/files/Apple-Pay-Platform-Integration-Guide.pdf)
- Each domain shown in Apple Pay must host Apple's verification file at `https://[DOMAIN_NAME]/.well-known/apple-developer-merchantid-domain-association`. If merchants take payments on pages hosted on the platform's own domain, "host this file on your domain also." For iframes, "the top-level domain must be registered and verified," and the iframe needs `allow="payment"`. — [Apple Pay Platform Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/download/files/Apple-Pay-Platform-Integration-Guide.pdf)
- When onboarding merchants through the Web Merchant Registration API, the platform "must also ensure these guidelines are being met by the merchant." This refers to the Apple Pay on the Web Acceptable Use Guidelines. — [Apple Pay Platform Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/download/files/Apple-Pay-Platform-Integration-Guide.pdf)
- An Apple Developer Forums post from an acquirer/PSP says: "We register each merchant using the Web Merchant Registration API as a Platform Integrator, with a unique partnerInternalMerchantIdentifier. Merchants do not have their own Apple Developer accounts." — [Apple Developer Forums thread 852767](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/852767) (search snippet only)

**Stripe domain registration (from search snippets of Stripe docs; full pages not fetchable)**
- Stripe's Apple Pay doc, per snippet: "If you use Payment Links or hosted Checkout, Apple Pay works with no additional configuration." — [Stripe: Apple Pay](https://docs.stripe.com/apple-pay.md?platform=web)
- Stripe's registration guide requires registering every domain and subdomain where Elements or Checkout's embeddable form shows Apple Pay: "You need to register each domain and subdomain that you use." — [Stripe: Register domains for payment methods](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/payment-methods/pmd-registration)
- Connect rule, per snippet: "When a user runs a charge on a domain, the domain must be registered for that user, whether that user is your platform or the connected account." For direct charges, the platform registers each connected account's domain through the API (not the Dashboard), using its own secret key with the `Stripe-Account` header. For destination charges or separate charges and transfers, it omits that header and registers the domain on the platform. — [Stripe: Register domains for payment methods](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/payment-methods/pmd-registration?dashboard-or-api=api)
- The Apple Pay page snippet says per-connected-account registration "isn't a requirement for connected accounts using other charge types" (only direct charges need it). — [Stripe: Apple Pay](https://docs.stripe.com/apple-pay.md?platform=web)
- Stripe tries to verify Apple domain ownership automatically during registration. The legacy Apple Pay domain API still works, but disabling must now go through the Payment Method Domain API. — [Stripe: Register domains for payment methods](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/payment-methods/pmd-registration)
- Stripe lists Connect as supported for Apple Pay, and Invoicing among the products that support Apple Pay. — [Stripe: Apple Pay](https://docs.stripe.com/apple-pay)

**Charge types: merchant of record, descriptors, disputes**
- Destination charges: "The on_behalf_of parameter determines the settlement merchant, which defaults to your platform account." Passing the connected account's ID makes it the settlement merchant. With certain exceptions, `on_behalf_of` is required when the platform and the connected account are in different regions. — [Stripe: Create destination charges](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/destination-charges.md?locale=en-US&platform=web&ui=stripe-hosted) (snippet)
- Disputes on destination charges are debited from the platform balance "with or without on_behalf_of". With destination charges, Checkout uses the platform's brand settings unless `on_behalf_of` is set. — [Stripe: Create destination charges](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/destination-charges.md?locale=en-US&platform=web&ui=stripe-hosted) (snippet)
- Direct charges: the connected account's balance absorbs the disputed amount and fee, and the platform is debited only if Stripe can't collect from the connected account (and where the platform is liable). Destination charges and separate charges and transfers automatically debit the platform. — [Stripe: Disputes on Connect platforms](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/disputes) (snippet)
- "With direct charges, transaction risk primarily affects connected accounts; with destination charges and separate charges and transfers, transaction risk primarily affects platforms." — [Stripe: Manage risk (Connect)](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/manage-risk) (snippet)
- Statement descriptor: a third-party comparison says the descriptor follows the connected account for direct charges and for destination charges with `on_behalf_of`, while dispute liability stays with the platform for destination charges. I could not verify this against Stripe's pages. — [DEV Community: what on_behalf_of changes](https://dev.to/mihirkanzariya/what-onbehalfof-actually-changes-on-a-stripe-destination-charge-and-what-it-does-not-3afm)
- Connected accounts can manage disputes on destination + `on_behalf_of` charges only if both `dispute_management` and `destination_on_behalf_of_charge_management` are enabled. — [Stripe: Disputes on Connect](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/disputes) (snippet)

### Inferences
- **Macoma's current flows need no Apple setup by Macoma.** Owners' customers pay on Stripe-hosted invoice pages (invoice.stripe.com), and owners pay Macoma through hosted Checkout. Both live on Stripe domains, where Stripe acts as the Apple Pay platform integrator under its own credentials. Macoma needs no Apple merchant ID, no verification file, and no per-owner domain registration. This matches Apple's model: hosted pages require the platform to use the Web Merchant Registration API, which Stripe evidently does. Stripe's documentation of this is confirmed for Payment Links and hosted Checkout. For hosted invoice pages it is inferred: Invoicing is listed as Apple Pay-capable, but I found no explicit "no configuration" statement.
- If Macoma later embeds the Payment Element or embedded Checkout on macoma.com (or a subdomain), it must register each such domain and subdomain. Direct charges on connected accounts mean registering per connected account, but for Macoma's own domain, which Stripe can verify once. Destination charges mean registering once, on the platform.
- Charge-type choice for owner invoices. If owners' invoices are created as direct charges on the owner's connected account (typical for Standard accounts and a "Macoma is software, not a seller" posture), the owner is merchant of record: their name shows on the statement and the Apple Pay sheet, and their balance takes disputes. That fits Apple's model of the sub-merchant as the seller. Destination charges make Macoma liable for every owner's disputes and refunds, a larger risk exposure for a platform of many small merchants. `on_behalf_of` moves the settlement merchant and descriptor but not liability.
- Macoma's own subscription is a platform-account charge (Macoma is the merchant), so it raises no Connect issue.

## Q2. Do Apple rules say anything specific about platforms or marketplaces taking Apple Pay for sub-merchants (per-sub-merchant domain registration, a "platform integration" program)?

### Takeaway
Yes. Apple runs a formal platform-integrator path: the Apple Pay Platform Integration Guide plus the gated Web Merchant Registration API. It registers each sub-merchant (with its domains, or with platform-hosted pages) under the platform's Payment Platform Integrator ID and shared certificates, each with its own partnerInternalMerchantIdentifier. This path covers the web only. Native in-app Apple Pay requires the app developer's own merchant ID and certificate.

### Cited Findings
- The Web Merchant Registration API "enables platform integrators such as payment-service providers and e-commerce platforms to register web merchants who want to offer Apple Pay on the web," with merchants registered "with their own website domains, or with web pages hosted by your platform." — [Apple: Web Merchant Registration API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applepaywebmerchantregistrationapi) (search snippet; the page body would not render on fetch)
- Endpoints: registerMerchant, unregisterMerchant (removes one or more domains for a merchant), and getDetails. — [Apple Pay Platform Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/download/files/Apple-Pay-Platform-Integration-Guide.pdf)
- Apple tells platforms to give merchants the verification file, hosting instructions, and a way to list the domains they want registered. — [Apple Pay Platform Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/download/files/Apple-Pay-Platform-Integration-Guide.pdf)
- Apple says platforms should support at least two active key pairs per configuration for rotation, and should flag Apple Pay transactions in merchant reporting. — [Apple Pay Platform Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/download/files/Apple-Pay-Platform-Integration-Guide.pdf)
- From Apple's guide: "Use Apple Pay in your app to sell physical goods ...; for services such as club memberships, hotel reservations, and events tickets; and for donations. Use In-App Purchases to sell virtual goods such as premium content for your app, and subscriptions for digital content." — [Apple Pay Platform Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/download/files/Apple-Pay-Platform-Integration-Guide.pdf)
- Other PSPs follow the same pattern. Payrix requires each merchant ID to be registered against the shared domain. Evolve and Xplor expose APIs to register and deregister domains per merchant. — [Payrix: Apple Pay registration](https://resource.payrix.com/docs/apple-pay-registration); [Evolve: Apple Pay merchant registration](https://docs.evolve.accesspaysuite.com/docs/apple-pay-merchant-registration); [Xplor Pay: Apple Merchant](https://docs.xplorpay.com/api-reference/payments/mobile/apple-merchant)

### Inferences
- Macoma does not need to join Apple's platform-integrator program while it uses Stripe-hosted pages or Stripe Elements. Stripe is the integrator, and Macoma uses Stripe's domain-registration API instead.
- Merchant identity on the Apple Pay sheet is per sub-merchant in Apple's model. This supports using direct charges, or `on_behalf_of`, for owner invoices so customers see the owner's name.

- I did not read the Apple Pay on the Web Acceptable Use Guidelines (linked from the guide) in full. They may restrict which merchant categories and use cases (e.g., P2P) may accept Apple Pay on the web.
- I did not research Adyen or Braintree platform docs, beyond the general PSP pattern above.

## Q3. App Store Review Guideline 3.1 and related rules: does Macoma's subscription need IAP in an iOS app? Can owners' invoices use Apple Pay in-app? What changed in 2025–2026 in the US and EU?

### Takeaway
Macoma's AI-assistant subscription is a digital service (SaaS) that unlocks app or service functionality. Under 3.1.1 and 3.1.2(a), selling it inside an iOS app requires In-App Purchase in every storefront. In the US storefront, however, the app may also link or button out to Macoma's web Stripe Checkout with no entitlement (since May 1, 2025). Apple currently charges no commission on those link-out purchases while litigation continues. The Supreme Court took Apple's appeal on a narrow question, and a remand will set what "costs-based" commission Apple may eventually charge. Outside the US, the app should either sell via IAP or carry no purchase calls to action, unless it uses a regional entitlement (the EU DMA terms changed again on October 1, 2026). Owners' invoices to their own customers (plumbers, salons, and other real-world services consumed outside the app) fall under 3.1.3(e). They must not use IAP and may use Apple Pay or card entry in-app.

### Cited Findings
**Guideline text (developer.apple.com, read Oct 31, 2026)**
- 3.1.1: "If you want to unlock features or functionality within your app, (by way of example: subscriptions, in-game currencies, game levels, access to premium content, or unlocking a full version), you must use in-app purchase." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.2(a) lists "software as a service ("SAAS"); and cloud support" as examples of appropriate auto-renewable subscriptions, which "must work on all of the user's devices where the app is available." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.1(a): developers may apply for entitlements to link to other purchase methods; "These entitlements are not required for developers to include buttons, external links, or other calls to action in their United States storefront apps." In all other storefronts without an entitlement, "apps and their metadata may not include buttons, external links, or other calls to action that direct customers to purchasing mechanisms other than in-app purchase." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.3 intro: apps in this section "cannot, within the app, encourage users to use a purchasing method other than in-app purchase, except for apps on the United States storefront and as set forth in 3.1.1(a) and 3.1.3(a). Developers can send communications outside of the app to their user base about purchasing methods other than in-app purchase." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.3(b) Multiplatform Services: apps "may allow users to access content, subscriptions, or features they have acquired in your app on other platforms or your web site ... provided those items are also available as in-app purchases within the app." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.3(c) Enterprise Services: apps "only sold directly by you to organizations or groups for their employees or students" may give access to previously purchased subscriptions; "Consumer, single user, or family sales must use in-app purchase." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.3(d) Person-to-Person Services: "real-time person-to-person services between two individuals (for example tutoring students, medical consultations, real estate tours, or fitness training)" may use non-IAP methods; "One-to-few and one-to-many real-time services must use in-app purchase." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.3(e) Goods and Services Outside of the App: "If your app enables people to purchase physical goods or services that will be consumed outside of the app, you must use purchase methods other than in-app purchase to collect those payments, such as Apple Pay or traditional credit card entry." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.3(f) Free Stand-alone Apps: free companion apps to a paid web-based tool "for VoIP, Cloud Storage, Email Services, or Web Hosting" need no IAP "provided there is no purchasing inside the app, or calls to action for purchase outside of the app." The list does not include AI assistants or SMS services. — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.3(g) Advertising Management Apps: non-IAP is allowed for apps whose sole purpose is buying and managing ad campaigns. — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Note on numbering: in the current guidelines, **3.1.5 is "Cryptocurrencies"**, not real-world goods. The real-world goods and services rule is 3.1.3(e), and person-to-person is 3.1.3(d). — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.2.1(vii): individuals may gift money to other individuals without IAP if it is optional and 100% goes to the receiver, but not if connected to digital content or services. — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- **Macoma subscription inside an iOS app.** It is an AI-assistant SaaS subscription bought by a small business owner, which is digital functionality under 3.1.1 and 3.1.2(a). It is not a 3.1.3(e) real-world service, not a 3.1.3(d) person-to-person service, and not in the 3.1.3(f) list (VoIP, cloud storage, email, web hosting). 3.1.3(c) covers only org-level sales "for their employees," not single-owner sales. Options:
  - **(a) "Login-only" companion app.** No purchase UI. Owners subscribe on the web (Stripe Checkout) and sign in. Read literally, 3.1.3(b) still requires the subscription to also be offered via IAP. In practice many business SaaS apps ship login-only, but that practice is not codified, so App Review rejection risk remains outside the US.
  - **(b) US storefront.** The app may show a "Subscribe on macoma.com" button or link to Stripe Checkout, with Apple Pay on the web page, with no entitlement and (for now) no Apple commission. IAP is not strictly required to be offered alongside it under the US text, though 3.1.3(b)'s "also available as IAP" wording is still in the guidelines. The commission could change after the district-court remand or the Supreme Court decision; Question 1 argument is likely in the 2026–27 term.
  - **(c) EU.** The app can use IAP, alternative payment processing inside the app (about 20% under the October 2026 terms), or a link-out entitlement, each with commissions under the new terms.
  - **(d) Rest of world.** IAP, or no calls to action at all. Regional entitlements (e.g., Korea, Japan, the Netherlands) are outside this research.
  - Emails and SMS sent outside the app may always point owners to web pricing (3.1.3 intro). This matters for Macoma, since its main channel is SMS.
- **Owners' invoices to their customers.** These pay for real-world services (plumbing, haircuts, etc.) "consumed outside of the app." Under 3.1.3(e), IAP must **not** be used, and Apple Pay or card entry is explicitly allowed. This holds whether the invoice is paid in an iOS app or on the hosted invoice page. Customers likely won't install Macoma's app anyway, since invoices go out by SMS link. If owners sell digital goods through invoices (e.g., online courses), 3.1.1 could apply to an in-app purchase flow.
- If the Macoma iOS app let owners take payment in person (e.g., Tap to Pay on iPhone, or Apple Pay in an owner-facing app), that is still 3.1.3(e).

## Q4. Are there Apple Pay requirements specific to iOS apps (PassKit entitlement, merchant ID in the app, HIG)?

### Takeaway
Native in-app Apple Pay cannot use Apple's web platform-registration path. The app developer (Macoma) needs its own Apple Developer Program membership, a Merchant ID, an Apple Pay Payment Processing Certificate (via a Stripe-generated CSR), and the Apple Pay capability/entitlement in Xcode tied to that merchant ID. Guideline 4.9 adds disclosure and branding rules, including specific disclosures for recurring payments.

### Cited Findings
- Merchant-managed onboarding (own Apple Developer Account) is the method that "Supports native in-app Apple Pay integrations". The Web Merchant Registration API "Cannot be used for native in-app integrations." — [Apple Pay Platform Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/download/files/Apple-Pay-Platform-Integration-Guide.pdf)
- Processors handle decryption via a per-merchant key pair. "The processor provides the public key via a Certificate Signing Request (CSR), which the merchant uploads to the Apple Developer Portal to generate a Payment Processing Certificate." — [Apple Pay Platform Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/download/files/Apple-Pay-Platform-Integration-Guide.pdf)
- Stripe iOS steps (per snippets): enroll in the Apple Developer Program, register a Merchant ID, create the Apple Pay certificate from the CSR Stripe generates in the Dashboard's iOS Certificate Settings (one CSR, one certificate), and add the Apple Pay capability in Xcode under Signing & Capabilities with the merchant ID. Stripe points to Apple's PassKit docs for recurring payments. — [Stripe: Apple Pay](https://docs.stripe.com/apple-pay)
- Guideline 4.9: apps using Apple Pay "must provide all material purchase information to the user prior to sale," must use Apple Pay branding and UI per the Apple Pay Marketing Guidelines and HIG, and for recurring payments must disclose the renewal term length and that it continues until canceled, what each period provides, the actual charges, and how to cancel. — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple recommends showing the Apple Pay button early (product or basket page) and using the system-provided button. — [Apple Pay Platform Integration Guide](https://developer.apple.com/download/files/Apple-Pay-Platform-Integration-Guide.pdf)

### Inferences
- If a Macoma iOS app ever shows native Apple Pay (PKPaymentRequest or Stripe PaymentSheet) for owner invoices on connected accounts, the Apple merchant ID in the app is Macoma's (platform's) merchant ID and certificate, uploaded to Macoma's Stripe platform account. The connected account is reached through the `Stripe-Account` header (direct charges) or `on_behalf_of`/destination. Owners would not need Apple accounts. Stripe docs presumably cover this, but I could not read them.
- A lower-effort alternative avoids PassKit and certificates entirely: open the Stripe-hosted invoice page or Checkout in Safari or SFSafariViewController. Apple Pay works there under Stripe's web credentials. For Macoma's own subscription, this is permitted only on the US storefront, or with a regional entitlement.
- Using Apple Pay (not IAP) for Macoma's own SaaS subscription inside the app would conflict with 3.1.1 and with Apple's guide ("Use In-App Purchases to sell ... subscriptions for digital content"). Apple Pay in-app is only appropriate for owners' real-world invoices.
