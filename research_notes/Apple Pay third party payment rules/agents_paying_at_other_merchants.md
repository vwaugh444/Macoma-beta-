# Can third parties or AI agents use Apple Pay to pay on a user's behalf at other merchants? (as of Oct 2026)

Research context: Macoma is an SMS AI assistant that prepares purchases for small-business owners. Today the owner taps Apple Pay on the merchant's own page. The question is whether an AI could trigger, store, reuse or proxy Apple Pay.

Method note: several hosts could not be fetched from this session (ENOTFOUND): docs.stripe.com, applepaypartners.apple.com (Apple's merchant-token page now redirects there), and cnbc.com. Apple's payment-token format reference page returned only its title. Findings about those pages rely on search-result snippets and are flagged. Several agentic-commerce sources found were vendor or aggregator explainers (eco.com, paz.ai, didit.me, for.you.com); they are flagged where used.

## 1. Can an app/agent capture, store, forward or reuse an Apple Pay payment token for a different merchant?

### Takeaway
No, not in any usable or permitted way. An Apple Pay token is encrypted to one merchant's payment-processing certificate (bound to that merchant's identifier), so only that merchant or its processor can decrypt it. Apple's web rules also ban "staged digital wallets" and any "substitute merchant of record," which is exactly what a proxy-and-repay model would be. The one legitimate "forwarding" pattern is a platform (for example Stripe's agentic handler) passing the encrypted token straight to the merchant it was created for.

### Cited Findings
- Apple creates the payment token at transaction time. The card data inside is encrypted with the public key from the merchant's payment processing certificate. The token header carries a `publicKeyHash` that identifies which merchant key was used, and the version field (EC_v1 / RSA_v1) gives the scheme. Decryption needs the merchant's private key (ECDH with the token's ephemeral public key, then a NIST SP 800-56A KDF). Integrators should verify the certificate chain and signature first. — [Basis Theory: Decrypt Apple Pay data with your own keys](https://developers.basistheory.com/docs/guides/apple-pay/own-encryption-keys); [Wpay: Tokenizing Apple Pay](https://developerhub.wpay.com.au/digitalpayments/docs/tokenizing-apple-pay); [Spreedly Apple Pay docs](https://developer.spreedly.com/docs/apple-pay); [Apple: Payment token format reference](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/passkit/payment-token-format-reference) (the Apple page itself only returned a title when fetched; the details come from the third-party integration guides)
- The merchant identifier uses reverse-domain naming (for example `merchant.com.yourdomain.pay`). The payment processing certificate is created under that Merchant ID in the Apple Developer portal. A separate merchant identity certificate authenticates calls to Apple's payment-session API. — [Basis Theory](https://developers.basistheory.com/docs/guides/apple-pay/own-encryption-keys); [Solidgate Apple Pay H2H](https://support.solidgate.com/en/support/solutions/articles/80001195146-apple-pay-h2h)
- Apple's Acceptable Use Guidelines for Apple Pay on the Web say a merchant "may not incorporate Apple Pay into a website that ... Constitutes a staged digital wallet (e.g., where a second payment transaction is conducted in order to complete the first transaction or where there is a substitute merchant of record in a transaction)." They also say "You may not use the Apple Pay for any purpose other than to enable or facilitate an Apple Pay transaction from your website," and "Apple reserves the right at any time to disable Apple Pay transactions on your websites for any reason it deems prudent." — [Apple: Acceptable Use Guidelines for Apple Pay on the Web](https://developer.apple.com/apple-pay/acceptable-use-guidelines-for-websites/)
- The same guidelines ban currency purchases or transfers, including crypto, unless Apple approves them. That would cover a "load a balance, then the agent spends it" design. — [Apple Acceptable Use Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/apple-pay/acceptable-use-guidelines-for-websites/)
- Apple's web terms allow a "Platform Provider" (a payments or e-commerce provider engaged by the merchant under a written agreement). The provider must hold an Apple developer account and accept a Platform Provider Addendum. "Website" is defined as one "developed by or on behalf of You, under Your name". So the platform acts for the merchant, not for the shopper. — [Apple Pay Platform Web Terms for Direct Merchants (Stripe-hosted copy)](https://stripe.com/apple-pay/legal); [Apple: apple-pay-web-direct-merchants terms](https://developer.apple.com/terms/apple-pay-web-direct-merchants)
- Apple's Web Merchant Registration API is for platform integrators (for example PSPs) that "manage Apple Pay configuration on the merchants' behalf." — [Apple: Apple Pay Web Merchant Registration API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applepaywebmerchantregistrationapi)
- An unverified (Google-Docs-hosted) "Merchant Terms and Conditions for Enabling Apple Pay" says merchants must not collect or store Apple Pay credentials or payment token data beyond what transaction processing needs. Authenticity is unverified. — [Google Doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/18V09dMAeSzbeAYZ1uo-twXyxQzeYYrotcDpWR5m0cRQ/export?format=pdf)
- Stripe's UCP (Universal Commerce Protocol) payments handler covers card and Apple Pay. Per the search snippet, for Apple Pay "agents receive an encrypted PKPaymentToken from Apple rather than raw card details." The platform serializes the token and sends it to the merchant's completion endpoint with the Stripe handler, and the result is a Shared Payment Token. (docs.stripe.com was not fetchable, so this is from the snippet only.) — [Stripe Payments handler for UCP](https://docs.stripe.com/agentic-commerce/protocols/ucp/stripe-payments-handler)

### Inferences
- Because the ciphertext is bound to merchant A's key, a token captured by Macoma (or by merchant A) cannot be decrypted or charged by merchant B. "Reuse at another merchant" is cryptographically impossible, not just against policy. To act as the decryptor, Macoma would have to be the merchant (its own Merchant ID and certificate). Then Macoma would be the merchant of record charging the owner and paying the supplier separately. That is the "staged digital wallet / substitute merchant of record" pattern Apple bans on the web, and it raises money-transmission questions (section 5).
- The Stripe UCP pattern suggests the sanctioned way to "forward" an Apple Pay token is: the agent or platform shows the Apple Pay sheet for the merchant's Merchant ID/domain, the user authenticates, and the encrypted token passes untouched to that merchant's processor. The agent carries the token but cannot read or reuse it.
- Apple Pay payment tokens carry a one-time cryptogram. My understanding (not verified from Apple's page here) is that they are meant for a single authorization, so storing one to replay later would not work even at the same merchant. Recurring use needs a merchant token (MPAN; see section 2).

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the full text of Apple's Payment Token Format Reference, the Apple Pay section of the Apple Developer Program License Agreement, or the Apple Pay Merchant/Platform agreements. So I have no verbatim clauses on token storage, single use, or signing-time freshness checks.
- Could not verify whether Apple's in-app (iOS) rules carry the same "staged digital wallet" ban as the web guidelines. It is likely, but not confirmed here.

## 2. Must every Apple Pay payment be authenticated on the user's device? Exceptions?

### Takeaway
Every customer-initiated Apple Pay purchase needs on-device Face ID, Touch ID or passcode, which no remote agent can supply. There are two narrow exceptions. Express Mode (transit, IDs, keys) skips biometrics at the reader. Merchant tokens (MPANs) let one specific merchant charge later, off-session, for recurring, automatic-reload or deferred payments the user approved once on-device. Neither lets a third party start a new purchase at a different merchant.

### Cited Findings
- Apple's Apple Pay page: every purchase requires Face ID, Touch ID or passcode. Card numbers aren't shared with merchants or stored on the device or Apple's servers. With Express Mode, "you don't need to authenticate your payment with Face ID or Touch ID" (transit). — [Apple: Apple Pay (AU)](https://apple.com/au/apple-pay)
- Express Mode is limited to transit cards, plus student IDs, home keys and other compatible cards (sources differ on exact scope). — [Apple: Apple Pay](https://apple.com/au/apple-pay); [OCTA Express Mode](https://octa.net/getting-around/bus/oc-bus/wave/ways-to-pay/contactless-payment/express-mode-with-apple-wallet); [SEPTA](https://wwww.septa.org/news/express-mode-iphone-and-apple-watch)
- Researchers showed Visa cards could be abused through Apple Pay Express Transit mode on a locked iPhone. This shows Express Mode is a deliberate, narrow trade-off. — [T3](https://t3.com/news/could-a-contactless-card-reader-steal-money-from-my-locked-iphone)
- Apple's Platform Security guide is the authoritative source on Apple Pay authorization (body text not retrieved). — [Apple Platform Security: Apple Pay](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/sec82e7bc3f8/web)
- Apple's description of merchant tokens: "In addition to device-specific payment tokens used to securely complete a single Apple Pay transaction, use Apple Pay merchant tokens to securely complete automatic reload or recurring payments independent of a device." (search snippet; the page now redirects to applepaypartners.apple.com, which was not fetchable) — [Apple: Apple Pay merchant tokens](https://developer.apple.com/apple-pay/merchant-tokens/)
- Merchant-token request types are `PKRecurringPaymentRequest` (scheduled, merchant-initiated) and `PKAutomaticReloadPaymentRequest` (top-up when a balance falls below a threshold). Charges must follow credential-on-file rules. Per Paysafe, a saved merchant token cannot be used for a new customer-initiated purchase. — [Paysafe: Apple Pay recurring](https://developer.paysafe.com/en/api-docs/payments-api/add-payment-methods/apple-pay/apple-pay-recurring-transactions/)
- The pattern is: save Apple Pay on-session (customer-initiated transaction, CIT), then charge later off-session (merchant-initiated transaction, MIT). MPANs survive device changes. — [Stripe: Apple Pay merchant tokens](https://docs.stripe.com/apple-pay/merchant-tokens) (snippet only); [MoneyHash: Apple Pay recurring](https://docs.moneyhash.io/docs/accept-apple-paytm-recurring-payments)
- Apple's privacy page: Apple knows which merchants are tied to your merchant-specific account numbers, but not what you bought. This confirms MPANs are merchant-specific. — [Apple Pay & Privacy](https://www.apple.com/za/legal/privacy/data/en/apple-pay/)
- Tap to Pay on iPhone is a merchant-acceptance product. Merchants must have an agreement with a PSP that Apple has authorized. It lets a merchant accept payments; it does not let a third party pay. — [Apple: Tap to Pay on iPhone terms (UK)](https://www.apple.com/legal/internet-services/business-services/tap-to-pay-on-iphone/en-gb.html)
- A Mastercard Agent Pay explainer describes an Apple Pay device token as assuming a human is present and biometrically authenticated at checkout, while agentic tokens assume the human pre-authorized the agent. (Third-party explainer, not Apple.) — [eco.com: What is Mastercard Agent Pay](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15192001-what-is-mastercard-agent-pay-ai-agent-commerce-protocol-in-2026)

### Inferences
- The only "AI pays later without a tap" route inside Apple Pay is a merchant token held by the merchant (for example a supplier that sets up recurring or auto-reload billing with the owner). The merchant initiates those charges, not the agent, and each merchant needs its own on-device opt-in.
- For Macoma, the current design (owner taps Apple Pay on the merchant's page) matches how Apple Pay is built. An agent can prepare the cart and deep-link to checkout, but the owner's on-device authentication is the gate.

### Gaps
- Could not get Apple's verbatim Platform Security text on "secure intent" (side-button double-click) or the exact Express Mode scope.
- Did not confirm the "deferred payment" merchant-token type (`PKDeferredPaymentRequest`) from a primary source in this session. It is believed to exist since iOS 17, but this is unverified here.

## 4. How do Visa Intelligent Commerce, Mastercard Agent Pay and Stripe's agent tools relate to Apple Pay; can a card in Apple Wallet be used for agent payments?

### Takeaway
The card-network agent programs are separate tokenization products: agent-specific network tokens bound to an agent or device and authenticated with passkeys. They are built on the same network token services (Visa VTS, Mastercard MDES) that power Apple Pay, but they are not Apple Pay and do not draw on the device token in Apple Wallet. A user's card can be enrolled for agent use through the issuer or network, separately from Apple Wallet. The Apple Wallet device token itself cannot be handed to an agent. The one confirmed overlap is Stripe's agentic handler, which can carry a user-authorized Apple Pay token to the specific merchant.

### Cited Findings
- Visa Intelligent Commerce (announced April 30, 2025) lets AI agents hold and use Visa credentials through agent-specific tokens and passkey authentication. Visa's developer page says the tokens are "bound to user devices and secured using Passkeys" and that the product is in development and not available in all markets. — [Visa Developer: Visa Intelligent Commerce for AI Agents](https://developer.visa.com/use-cases/visa-intelligent-commerce-for-agents); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/visa/2025/visa-powers-ai-shopping-agents-with-intelligent-commerce-payment-rails)
- Visa Intelligent Commerce Connect offers one integration for payment initiation, tokenization, spend controls and authentication in agent transactions. — [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/visa-launches-intelligent-commerce-connect-for-agentic-payments)
- Mastercard Agentic Tokens (Agent Pay, April 29, 2025) are framed as an extension of the same MDES tokenization used for Apple Pay and Google Pay. Agent Pay is being built into PayPal's checkout wallet. A June 2026 "Agent Pay for machines" rollout has 30+ partners. — [eco.com](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15192001-what-is-mastercard-agent-pay-ai-agent-commerce-protocol-in-2026); [PYMNTS: Mastercard and PayPal](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=3168054); [Genfinity](https://genfinity.io/2026/06/10/mastercard-agent-pay-for-machines-launch/)
- Stripe's Agentic Commerce Suite includes Shared Payment Tokens (SPTs), which "let AI agents securely pass a buyer's payment credentials to businesses for processing payments." — [Stripe newsroom: Agentic Commerce Suite](https://stripe.com/newsroom/news/agentic-commerce-suite)
- In ChatGPT Instant Checkout (Agentic Commerce Protocol), the SPT is scoped to a specific merchant and cart total. The Delegated Payment spec makes the token single-use, capped by a max amount and an expiry. Sources conflict on Apple Pay: one says users can pay with Apple Pay, another says Apple Pay support was planned or added later. — [ACP Delegated Payment spec](https://agentic-commerce-protocol.pages.dev/docs/commerce/specs/payment); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/ai-story/openai-instant-checkout-agentic-commerce-protocol-ai); [TestingCatalog: Instacart in ChatGPT](https://www.testingcatalog.com/instacart-launches-instant-grocery-checkout-inside-chatgpt)
- Stripe's UCP payments handler covers "card and Apple Pay" (Google Pay uses its own handler). An Apple Pay PKPaymentToken is passed encrypted to the merchant's completion endpoint. (Snippet only; docs.stripe.com was not fetchable.) — [Stripe UCP handler](https://docs.stripe.com/agentic-commerce/protocols/ucp/stripe-payments-handler)
- One explainer compares one-time-use cards (Stripe Issuing virtual cards) with SPTs for agent commerce. (Third-party blog.) — [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/one-time-use-cards-vs-shared-payment-tokens-stripe-agent-commerce)

### Inferences
- For Macoma there are three Apple-compliant paths. (a) Keep the owner's on-device Apple Pay tap, with the agent preparing the cart, which is the current design. (b) Where merchants support ACP/UCP via Stripe, show an Apple Pay sheet in Macoma's own surface (app or web) for that merchant and pass the encrypted token on. This needs a Macoma app or web surface; it cannot work over SMS alone, because Apple Pay authorization happens on the device. (c) For true no-tap agent payments, use non-Apple-Pay rails: Stripe Issuing virtual cards owned by the business, SPTs from a saved card, or network agentic tokens (Visa VIC / Mastercard Agent Pay) as they reach general availability. In (c) the card is the same physical account the owner has in Apple Wallet, but it is enrolled separately, not through Apple Pay.
- Option (c) with Issuing cards makes Macoma or the business the cardholder, which avoids the pay-on-behalf token problem. Spend controls and per-merchant single-use cards make good guardrails.

## 5. Legal and regulatory constraints for an assistant that pays on a user's behalf (money transmission, consent, EU DMA NFC)

### Takeaway
Apple's own rules (no substitute merchant of record, no staged wallets, no unapproved currency transfers) line up with the money-transmission risk. If Macoma takes funds from the owner and pays suppliers, it would likely be a money transmitter or need a licensed partner. The EU DMA NFC commitments open iPhone NFC/HCE tap-to-pay to third-party wallets in the EEA, but that is for in-store contactless wallets. It does not give remote or online access to Apple Pay tokens and does not help an SMS agent. Network rules on credential-on-file and merchant-initiated transactions (MIT) need explicit stored consent.

### Cited Findings
- On July 11, 2024 the European Commission made Apple's NFC commitments binding for 10 years, monitored by a trustee. EEA third-party wallet developers get free NFC/HCE access, default-payment-app settings, access to Face ID-style authentication, and a suppression mechanism, without using Apple Pay or Wallet. After market testing, Apple dropped a PSP-licence requirement and extended HCE to certified terminals such as SoftPOS. Apple said Apple Pay and Apple Wallet would not change. — [European Commission press release IP/24/3706](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/fin/ip_24_3706/IP_24_3706_EN.pdf); [Law Society Gazette](https://www.lawsociety.ie/gazette/top-stories/2024/july/eu-accepts-fresh-apple-wallet-pledges/); [Payments Industry Intelligence](https://paymentsindustryintelligence.com/?p=53352)
- Some 2026-dated pages (iClarified Feb 5, 2026; IEU Monitoring Jul 6, 2026) appear to restate the 2024 decision. No separate 2026 NFC action was confirmed. — [iClarified](https://www.iclarified.com/94191/eu-and-apple-reach-agreement-on-nfc-access-for-mobile-payments-on-iphone); [IEU Monitoring](https://ieu-monitoring.com/editorial/antitrust-eu-commission-seeks-feedback-on-commitments-offered-by-apple-over-practices-related-to-apple-pay/426094/)
- Apple Acceptable Use Guidelines ban sites that involve "the purchase or transfer of currency ... unless approved by Apple" and staged digital wallets with a substitute merchant of record. — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/apple-pay/acceptable-use-guidelines-for-websites/)
- Merchant-token recurring and reload charges must follow card-network credential-on-file rules, with the first charge customer-initiated and later charges merchant-initiated. — [Paysafe](https://developer.paysafe.com/en/api-docs/payments-api/add-payment-methods/apple-pay/apple-pay-recurring-transactions/); [MoneyHash](https://docs.moneyhash.io/docs/accept-apple-paytm-recurring-payments)
- Visa/Artemis research notes there is no settled way to reverse an agent payment that goes wrong; chargeback rules were built for human-speed commerce. — [Visa: Agentic payments from the ground up](https://www.visa.com/en-us/thought-leadership/innovation/agentic-payments-from-the-ground-up)
- The IMF published a 2026 note on how agentic AI will reshape payments. (Not reviewed in detail.) — [IMF Notes 2026/004](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/068/2026/004/article-A001-en.xml)

### Inferences
- Proxy model (Macoma charges the owner via Apple Pay as merchant of record, then pays the supplier) carries three risks: (1) it breaks Apple's substitute-merchant and staged-wallet rule; (2) it likely triggers US state money-transmitter licensing or EU PSD2 payment-institution rules unless an exemption applies (for example agent-of-payee or commercial-agent) or a licensed partner (such as Stripe Connect or Issuing) holds the funds; (3) it hides the real merchant from card-network dispute rights. This is legal analysis and not verified in this session; get counsel.
- Consent: an agent that triggers payments needs recorded, scoped authorization, meaning amount limits, merchant, and expiry, as ACP's delegated-payment caps and network agentic-token "mandates" (AP2) show. SMS-only confirmation ("reply YES") is not Apple Pay authentication and would not satisfy Apple Pay's on-device requirement.

### Gaps
- Found no primary source on US money-transmission treatment of AI purchasing agents, or on CFPB/Reg E/Reg Z positions about agent-initiated transactions in 2025–2026. This needs a dedicated legal search.
- Found no source on EU PSD3/PSR provisions for agent-initiated payments or SCA delegation to AI agents.
